# OWR Parent Hub — Content Direction

2 Nov 2026 · Eric Han

## Direction

Write fewer guides, and pick only topics where tonight's parent script and a teen's chapter fit the same moment. Start with Discipline. It has 7 adjacent guides, but none covers the book's two headline situations: a phone eating the whole evening, and giving up after one missed day.

The slate, ranked after the panel test and council:

1. **Phone eats the whole evening:** take back the evening without confiscating the phone (Discipline)
2. **Restarting after a missed day:** when one lapse becomes "I give up" (Discipline)
3. **Gift books for teens who don't read:** seasonal, ships before the Christmas cut-off (all three books)
4. **Teen says a teacher is unfair:** challenge a rule without blowing up (War)
5. **Comparing themselves online:** build a scoreboard of their own (War)
6. **Son watching "alpha" influencers:** strength without cruelty, without a ban (War)
7. **How manipulators hook teens online:** a cybersecurity dad's field guide (War + Social); ships only after a safety review
8. **Lead, don't manage:** becomes the Parent Hub's start-here page, not a guide

Folded into book pages instead of new guides: "Is The Art of War OK for teens?" and "Stoicism for exam stress". Both answer a buyer's doubt, so they belong in the book page FAQ.

### Rules every new guide follows

1. **Book relevance is direct.** Each topic maps to a situation or chapter the Playbook already covers. The bridge passes v4 §16 because it is the teen's half of the same problem.
2. **Parent's half first, teen's half last.** Open with what the parent says or does tonight. End with the optional chapter the teen can choose, linking to that book page's excerpt, not the homepage grid.
3. **Title in the parent's words.** The situation as typed at 11pm. No doctrine words (Release, capability) and never "war" in a parent-facing title.
4. **One values line per guide.** Strength without cruelty, influence without manipulation, or discipline without self-hatred, from the series moral rule.
5. **Plain English, wide families.** Short sentences for parents reading in a second language. Examples include family duties such as sibling care and part-time work, not only the teen's own goals.
6. **Skill, not character.** Discipline topics say the struggle is a learnable skill, name ADHD and anxiety as reasons to adjust, and say when to get support.
7. **One named source per major claim.** GEO extraction and parent trust both need it.

- **No guide explains a classic source.** Sun Tzu, the Stoics and Carnegie appear only on book pages. What the panel rejected, and why:

- **"Is The Art of War OK for teens?"** P2 rates it 5 and P4 and P5 rate it 4, but P1, P6 and P7 rate it 2: "war" reads as aggression before the page can explain it. It moves to the War book page FAQ, where a parent already considering the book meets it.
- **"Stoicism for exam stress"** has one champion (P4) and is abstract for the rest. Its answer joins the Discipline book page.
- **"Influence without manipulation"** scores lowest (2.8): parents don't search for the principle, they search for the threat. Its substance lives inside the manipulation guide.
- **"The wisdom our generation missed"** is a brand story, not a search. It becomes welcome email 2.

Objections that cut across topics, now written into the rules above: doctrine words and "war" in titles (P1, P7), individualism that ignores family duty (P6), idioms and slang (P5), willpower shaming (P8), and a hub with almost no daughters (P1, P4).

The final order is not the score order. The manipulation guide scores second but ships last because of its safety gate and softer book bridge, and "Lead, don't manage" scores well but works better as the hub's front page than as one more guide.

## Topic slate

Six briefs below map one-to-one onto `PUBLISH.json` fields, so Hermes can open a bundle from each without re-deciding anything. Two items are site pages, not guides, because Hermes does not edit templates or book pages. Claims marked "verify" need a fetched source before drafting. Each seo_title is 30–40 characters because the site appends " | Old Wisdom Retold" and the SEO gate wants 50–60 in total.

### 1. Phone eats the whole evening

- **slug:** `phone-takes-whole-evening`
- **title:** When the Phone Takes the Whole Evening: Help Your Teen Take It Back Without Confiscating It
- **seo_title:** Teen on Phone All Evening? What Works
- **target prompt:** "My 13-year-old is on her phone from dinner to midnight and we fight about it every night. What actually works?"
- **journey_stage:** GUIDE · supports REFLECT, RELEASE
- **teen_outcomes:** capability, ownership
- **territory:** confidence (v4: independence and responsibility)
- **belief shift:** from "If I don't take the phone, nothing changes" → to "A plan for the first 30 minutes after dinner that my teen designs and we review weekly can do more than nightly confiscation."
- **release move:** parent keeps shared-space and sleep rules and models them; the teen designs and runs their own evening start.
- **book bridge:** Discipline Playbook, situation "A phone or video keeps taking the whole evening" → `/books/the-discipline-playbook#sample`
- **next step:** `task-start-runway`
- **evidence:** about 4 in 10 parents and teens argue regularly about phone time; 46% of teens say a parent is at least sometimes distracted by their own phone (Pew 2024)
- **values line:** discipline without self-hatred
- **panel:** highest score with all eight. Add an ADHD note for P8, shared devices and space for P6, and study time for P5.

### 2. Restarting after a missed day

- **slug:** `teen-gives-up-after-missed-day`
- **title:** When One Missed Day Turns Into "I Give Up": Teach Your Teen to Restart
- **seo_title:** Teen Gives Up After a Missed Day?
- **target prompt:** "My son quits every new habit the first time he misses a day. How do I help him stop being all-or-nothing?"
- **journey_stage:** REFLECT · supports GUIDE, RELEASE
- **teen_outcomes:** judgment, ownership, independence
- **territory:** confidence
- **belief shift:** from "Missing a day proves he isn't serious" → to "The skill is the restart, and my reaction to the lapse decides whether he tries again."
- **release move:** the teen chooses a restart rule; the parent asks one neutral question instead of giving a verdict.
- **book bridge:** Discipline Playbook, situation "You missed a day and feel like giving up the whole plan" → `#sample`
- **next step:** `reliability-signal`
- **evidence:** verify Lally et al. (2010) on missed days during habit formation before citing
- **values line:** discipline without self-hatred
- **panel:** strongest for P8, P1, P4 and P5. Use sport training and game streaks as examples so P2 and P3 see their sons in it.

### 3. Teen says a teacher is unfair

- **slug:** `teen-says-teacher-unfair`
- **title:** Your Teen Says a Teacher Is Unfair: Help Them Raise It Respectfully
- **seo_title:** Teen Says a Teacher Is Unfair?
- **target prompt:** "My 15-year-old says his teacher marks him unfairly and wants to argue in class. Do I email the school or let him handle it?"
- **journey_stage:** GUIDE · supports RELEASE
- **teen_outcomes:** judgment, capability, independence
- **territory:** conflict
- **belief shift:** from "If it's unfair, I need to step in" → to "My teen can check the rule, gather facts and ask in a way that makes yes easy. I step in for safety, discrimination, or when the routine route fails."
- **release move:** the teen owns the fact-check and the first conversation; the parent keeps clear re-entry thresholds.
- **book bridge:** War Playbook, "Smart rebels read the rulebook", "Make yes easy" and "Aim at the issue, not the ego" → `/books/the-war-playbook#sample`
- **next step:** `teen-wont-ask-teacher-for-help`
- **values line:** strength without cruelty
- **panel:** top for P8. The word "respectfully" in the title is what keeps P5, who expects respect for teachers.

### 4. Comparing themselves online

- **slug:** `teen-compares-self-online`
- **title:** When Your Teen Compares Themselves to Everyone Online
- **seo_title:** Teen Comparing Themselves Online?
- **target prompt:** "After scrolling, my daughter says everyone else is prettier and doing better. How do I respond without dismissing it?"
- **journey_stage:** UNDERSTAND · supports GUIDE
- **teen_outcomes:** judgment, ownership
- **territory:** confidence
- **belief shift:** from "Telling her she's great will fix it" → to "Reassurance alone rarely holds. Helping her choose what she measures herself on does more."
- **book bridge:** War Playbook, "Your advantage is where you stopped comparing" → `#sample`
- **next step:** `signs-of-real-teen-confidence`
- **evidence:** social media is the most-cited concern among Australian parents of 12–18s at 59% (ReachOut 2024); find a peer-reviewed social comparison source when drafting
- **values line:** confidence without arrogance
- **panel:** top for P1, the daughter case the hub lacks. Include boys' versions (gym, game rank). Name body image and eating concerns as a reason to get professional help.

### 5. Son watching "alpha" influencers

- **slug:** `son-watching-alpha-influencers`
- **title:** Your Son Is Watching "Alpha" Influencers: What to Say Instead of Banning Them
- **seo_title:** Son Watching Alpha Influencers?
- **target prompt:** "My 14-year-old keeps quoting alpha-male videos. Banning it will make it worse. What do I say?"
- **journey_stage:** UNDERSTAND · supports REFLECT, GUIDE
- **teen_outcomes:** judgment, ownership
- **territory:** influence
- **belief shift:** from "He's being radicalised and I must shut it down" → to "He's looking for a map of how to be strong. I can offer a better one and keep him talking."
- **book bridge:** War Playbook, "Noise is not power" and the series rule against alpha dominance language → `#sample`
- **next step:** `guide-teen-without-power-struggle`
- **evidence:** 33.2% of US boys 13–17 followed Andrew Tate at least a little (Scharrer, Ward and Volling; cite the journal, not the news write-up)
- **values line:** strength without cruelty
- **panel:** top for P3, strong for P2, P7 and P8. The hinge for dads is that it never sneers at wanting to be strong. Keep it to behaviour and values; avoid claims about named people beyond the cited study.

### 6. How manipulators hook teens online

- **slug:** `how-manipulators-target-teens-online`
- **title:** How Manipulators Hook Teenagers Online: A Cybersecurity Dad's Field Guide
- **seo_title:** How Manipulators Target Teens Online
- **target prompt:** "How do scammers and predators manipulate teenagers online, and how do I teach my son to spot it without scaring him?"
- **journey_stage:** SEE · supports GUIDE
- **teen_outcomes:** judgment, independence
- **territory:** reading
- **belief shift:** from "If I monitor everything, I'll catch it" → to "A teen who knows the pressure patterns (urgency, secrecy, flattery, isolation) and knows they won't be shamed for asking is better placed to spot what monitoring misses."
- **release move:** the parent keeps a no-shame reporting promise and the emergency steps; the teen owns spotting the pattern.
- **book bridge:** War Playbook Part Five, "Read people and protect position"; the Social Playbook's Empathy Test separates influence from manipulation
- **next step:** `should-i-check-my-teens-phone`
- **evidence:** the FBI received 13,000+ reports of financial sextortion of minors, at least 12,600 victims, mostly boys 14–17, Oct 2021–Mar 2023 ([FBI, 2024](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/sacramento/news/sextortion-a-growing-threat-preying-upon-our-nations-teens)); 21% of NZ adults were personally targeted or know someone who was ([Netsafe, 2025](https://resource.netsafe.org.nz/Netsafe-Sextortion-Report-October-2025.pdf))
- **values line:** influence without manipulation
- **gate:** safety review before publishing. Reporting routes for each country, verified when drafting; no method detail that helps an offender; suicide-safe messaging with no link to suicide in the headline or summary.
- **panel:** second-highest score across all eight, and it is the topic only Eric can write. It ranks 6th here because the book bridge is the softest of the six.

### Site pages, not guides

- **Gift books for teens who don't read** (`/books/books-for-teens-who-dont-like-reading`): how to choose a book a reluctant reader finishes; the three Playbooks with "fits / doesn't fit" lines; 4–6 books by other authors that Eric has actually read, with a plain "I wrote three of these" disclosure. The NLT 20-year low is the hook. Check each Amazon store's Christmas delivery cut-off.
- **Lead, don't manage** (start-here block on `/parents/`): one screen in plain English. Managing means carrying the teen's tasks; leading means setting conditions, modelling, and handing decisions back. Then three first guides, one per book domain. For P5 and P6 it must read as leading with authority, not giving it up.

## Council review

Under review: eight new content pieces built to sit next to the books, chosen to serve both parent value and Amazon purchase clicks. Five advisors wrote independently, then read each other.

### 1. The Refuter

- **This is the named failure mode again.** Forty guides are live with no measured traffic. Eight more pieces before week 1's baseline is more assets before distribution.
- **The panel is Claude scoring Claude's topics.** Constructed parents find the objections a writer anticipates, not the ones real parents raise. Treat scores as a checklist, not demand.
- **Topic 6 carries real risk.** Sextortion content written badly can shame victims or teach method. One error costs more trust than the topic earns.
- **Seasonal pages decay.** A gift guide written after mid-November misses the shipping window and sits stale until next year.

### 2. The First-Principles Questioner

- Does a guide that fully solves the parent's problem make the book less needed? The "parent's half, teen's half" rule assumes parents want a tool for the teen as well. Nothing tests that yet.
- Is "complementary to the books" the same as "drives purchase clicks"? Only if the bridge sits at the moment the parent feels the teen needs their own tool.
- Which of these does anyone search for? Search Console is not connected, so no query data supports any title.
- Why should a parent trust Eric on discipline or teachers? The cybersecurity angle has authority; the others rely on the dad story.

### 3. The Opportunity Finder

- **The Discipline cluster turns three old guides into a pillar.** Homework, task start and reliability plus topics 1 and 2 make a five-guide hub around the newest book, at the cost of two new articles.
- **Topic 6 is a media hook.** Netsafe publishes NZ data and NZ outlets cover it; a cybersecurity dad with a field guide is a quotable source, which feeds the GEO off-site plan.
- **Folding the Art of War and Stoicism questions into book page FAQs** answers the buyer's doubt where the buy button is, with no new guide.
- **Daughters and blended families** are open lanes; topic 4 and the start-here page reach them first.

### 4. The Layperson

- "Stoicism", "Release", "capability": I don't know these words. I know "my kid is on the phone all night".
- Why would I click a book link at the end of an article that already helped me?
- Is the gift guide just an ad for your books? Only if it hides that you wrote them.
- "Lead, don't manage" sounds like a business slogan. Show me what it looks like at dinner.

### 5. The Ruthless Executor

- **Ship two guides, not six, before the data arrives:** topics 1 and 2, as one Discipline cluster with bridges, in week 3.
- **The gift page ships in week 2** or not at all this year.
- **Topic 6 waits** for a safety checklist and a verified reporting route for each of the US, UK, AU and NZ.
- **Cut:** a standalone "influence without manipulation" guide (it lives inside topic 6) and the "wisdom our generation missed" essay (it becomes welcome email 2).

### Cross-review

- The Executor's two-guide limit answers the Refuter's failure-mode point and still tests the Questioner's cannibalisation worry: compare book-page clicks from the two new bridged guides with the three existing bridged guides.
- The Layperson's vocabulary point is already rule 3; everyone accepts it.
- The Opportunity Finder's media hook survives only with the Refuter's safety gate. Both conditions stay attached to topic 6.
- No advisor defends the panel scores as evidence. They stay as an objection checklist.

### Chair's verdict

1. **Fix, then do.** The slate is right; the pace is wrong. Ship the gift page in week 2 and the Discipline pair in week 3, then decide the rest on week 3's numbers.
2. **Biggest risk:** writing six guides before knowing whether guide readers click to a book page at all.
3. **Missing evidence:** search queries (Search Console), clicks from guides to book pages, and real parent wording to replace the constructed panel.
4. **Smallest step today:** open the bundle folder for topic 1 with a draft `PUBLISH.json` filled from its brief, status `draft`.
5. **Confidence:** about 55% that the two Discipline guides beat the existing bridged guides on book-page clicks; about 30% that the simulated scores match real parent preference.
