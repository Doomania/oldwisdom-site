Somewhere right now, a parent is teaching their child how rooms work. How to read people. How to speak so others listen. How to walk into any situation and own it. They didn't learn it at school. Nobody does.

Sun Tzu, Aristotle, Marcus Aurelius, Carnegie — the sharpest minds in human history decoded influence, strategy, and human nature. Successful families have quietly applied this wisdom for generations. It's why some kids seem to just "get it" — and others spend years figuring out what they were never taught.

Your teen doesn't have to be one of the ones who figures it out too late.

NOT JUST FOR TEENS

MOST ADULTS ARE
STILL MISSING
THESE SKILLS

Here's the thing nobody says out loud: the social gaps that hold teens back don't disappear at 18. They follow people into careers, marriages, and leadership roles. The adult who struggles to be heard in meetings, who avoids conflict until it explodes, who can't quite connect with the people they need — they're still running the same patterns from adolescence.

The Social Playbook works for adults too. And there's something powerful about a parent reading it alongside their teen — not just to understand what they're learning, but to close the same gaps in themselves, at the same time.

The best thing you can give your teen isn't a book. It's a parent who's applying the same principles.

Career impact — still underestimated
Influence, strategic communication, and reading rooms determine who gets promoted. Technical skill is rarely the differentiator past a certain level.
Conflict patterns run deep
Most adult conflict — in relationships, at work, with family — traces to underdeveloped skills formed in adolescence. This is where they get corrected.
Practice together, compound together
When parent and teen work from the same framework, every dinner conversation becomes a low-stakes rehearsal. The learning multiplies.

Parent Questions, Answered

What is Old Wisdom // Retold?

A non-fiction series for teens 12–17 rebuilding classic wisdom into game-style playbooks. 30 short chapters per book, built for kids who don't finish books.

What age is The Social Playbook for?

12–17. No dating content, no swearing, 5–8 minute chapters. Plenty of parents read it too.

Is The Art of War appropriate for a 12-year-old?

The War Playbook teaches composure and which fights to skip — bullies, pile-ons, rivalries. Zero violence glorification.

How is this different from How to Win Friends and Influence People?

Same principles, teen-native examples: group chats and ranked lobbies instead of boardrooms.
