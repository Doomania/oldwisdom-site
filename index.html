THE SKILLS BETWEEN THE SUBJECTS

LEARNABLE SKILLS

LEFT TO CHANCE

School teaches subjects. Teen life also asks for judgment: how to enter a conversation, read the room, hold a boundary, choose a conflict and recover after a mistake. Those moments are often left to trial and error.

These are not personality verdicts. They are practical skills that can be noticed, practised, reviewed and improved.

OWR gives teens clear language and small reps. The aim is not to win at people. It is to act with more awareness, composure and choice.

HOW TO USE THE PLAYBOOKS

HAND OVER THE TOOL.
KEEP THE CHOICE WITH THE TEEN.

The Playbooks are written to the teen. They work best as an available tool — not an assignment, diagnosis or hidden parenting programme.

WHAT HELPS

  • Offer a choice of Playbook or chapter around a real challenge.
  • Let your teen read privately, use their own words and ignore what does not fit.
  • Ask permission before discussing a chapter — “Was anything useful?” is enough.
  • Let the teen choose one small rep and decide what the result means.

WHAT TO AVOID

  • Assigning a chapter as correction after a mistake.
  • Quoting the book back during an argument.
  • Using an idea to label, diagnose or test your teen.
  • Turning every rep into a progress report or grade.

The Playbook belongs to the teen. Parent Hub is where adults do their own learning.

Parent Questions, Answered

What is Old Wisdom // Retold?

Old Wisdom // Retold is a teen-growth platform. Its Playbooks turn durable ideas from strategy, philosophy and psychology into short practices for teens aged 12–17. Its separate Parent Hub helps adults support growth without taking ownership away.

Are the Playbooks written for parents?

No. The Playbooks speak directly to teens aged 12–17. Parent education lives separately in the Parent Hub, where adults can learn how to guide without turning a teen's book into an assignment, diagnosis or monitoring tool.

How should a parent introduce a Playbook?

Offer it as an optional tool around a real challenge, then step back. Let the teen choose what to read, what to try and whether to discuss it. Do not assign chapters after mistakes, quote the book during arguments or grade the outcome.

Is The War Playbook appropriate for a 12-year-old?

Yes. It reframes Sun Tzu around composure, de-escalation, boundaries and choosing which conflicts not to enter. It does not glorify violence.

Does Old Wisdom // Retold teach manipulation?

No. Every strategy should preserve dignity, choice and the possibility of a shared win: strength without cruelty, confidence without arrogance, influence without manipulation and strategy without dehumanising people.
