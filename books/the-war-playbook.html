Sun Tzu's The Art of War, adapted for everyday decisions

The manuscript identifies The Art of War as its source. Its first section uses the principle of knowing yourself and the situation before acting. The modern message exchange below is Eric Han's example, rather than a quotation from Sun Tzu.

Eric Han adapts the ideas through contemporary situations and practical exercises. The excerpt below is his writing from the manuscript.

Chapter 1 — Don't Fight Blind

Your phone buzzes.

“I heard what you said about me.”

Your stomach drops. Your face gets hot. Your thumb is already hovering over the keyboard.

You don’t know what they heard. You don’t know who told them. You don’t know if it’s true. You don’t even know if this is a real accusation or just bait.

[…]

You slow down. You ask one calm question.

“What exactly did you hear?”

Then you shut up and wait.

Selected passages from The War Playbook, Chapter 1 — Don't Fight Blind. Gaps between selected passages are marked with […].

Try the idea today

When a message feels loaded, pause and ask one calm question to clarify what actually happened. Bring threats or safety concerns to a trusted adult.

The practice above is a website introduction to the idea. The book contains the full chapter and its exercises.
