# Old Wisdom // Retold

## Purpose

Old Wisdom // Retold turns durable ideas from classic strategy, philosophy, and human psychology into practical, game-style playbooks that teens will actually use. The platform should grow as a global, evergreen book and education system rather than a client-service business.

## Primary Audience

- Parents of quiet, introverted, socially anxious, or socially struggling teens aged 12–17. They are the primary buyers.
- Teens aged 12–17 who want practical help with connection, confidence, influence, conflict, and social pressure. They are the primary users.
- Adults who recognise the same gaps in themselves are a secondary audience, especially when learning alongside a teen.

## Core Problem

School teaches academic knowledge but rarely teaches the practical social skills that shape friendships, confidence, leadership, and later career outcomes. Existing classics are dense, dated, and disconnected from teen life. Generic self-help advice is too abstract to apply under pressure.

## Product Promise

Give a parent and teen a calm, usable system for understanding a specific social gap, choosing one strategy, and practising it in real life without lectures, shame, or manipulation.

## Existing Product System

- The Social Playbook: connection, conversation, reading people, and ethical influence.
- The War Playbook: conflict, pressure, bullies, rivalries, and strategic composure.
- The Discipline Playbook: habits and self-mastery; currently coming soon.
- A free 15-question diagnostic with teen and parent paths, personalised profiles, gap analysis, and Brevo email capture.
- A free Chapter 1 email lead magnet.

## Platform Direction

- The Playbooks remain entirely teen-facing.
- Parent education lives outside the books in the Parent Growth content layer.
- Parent content helps readers see, understand, reflect, guide, and release so teens develop judgment, capability, ownership, and independence.
- Books may appear as relevant tools, but they are not the mandatory destination of every parent guide.

## Primary Journey

1. A parent discovers an evergreen article, video, pin, search result, or shared quiz result.
2. They land on a concern-specific page or the diagnostic.
3. The diagnostic identifies the three weakest skill areas and captures the email with consent.
4. The result recommends one clear next step matched to the weakest gap.
5. The reader receives a useful next step: another guide, a reflection, the quiz, or a relevant Playbook.
6. Brevo follows up with value-first lessons and longer-term evergreen education.
7. Campaign links preserve attribution so useful topics and channels can be evaluated.

## Voice

- Calm older brother/dad, not guru or therapist.
- Direct, specific, practical, and respectful of both parent and teen.
- Strategy language is welcome; fear, elitist posturing, and manipulation claims are not.
- No promises of guaranteed social, clinical, educational, or financial outcomes.

## Product Boundaries

- The diagnostic is informational, not a clinical or psychological assessment.
- No violence glorification, dating content, swearing, dark patterns, fake scarcity, fake testimonials, or fabricated results.
- Do not imply that introversion is a defect.
- Never expose private quiz responses in URLs or public analytics.
- Parent content must not turn the Playbooks into parenting manuals or force a product CTA into every article.

## Accessibility and Quality

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, semantics, touch targets, and motion preferences.
- Mobile is a first-class experience.
- Core content and conversion paths must work without animation.
- Preserve fast static delivery and avoid unnecessary dependencies.
