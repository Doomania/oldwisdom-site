## Brief claim audit

| Content claim used in article | Support | Wording guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Time alone or quietness by itself does not establish loneliness. | Mental Health America; ReachOut Parents | Presented as a reason to ask about the teen’s experience, not as proof that withdrawal is harmless. |
| A marked change from baseline, withdrawal from previously enjoyed people or activities, distress, and reduced daily functioning deserve closer attention. | HealthyChildren.org / AAP; ReachOut Parents | Framed as signs to look closer and seek qualified help where appropriate, not as a diagnosis. |
| A calm, non-assumptive conversation is a suitable first step when safety is not immediately at issue. | Mental Health America; ReachOut Parents | Offered as one parent move, not a guaranteed solution. |
| Persistent or severe distress and safety concerns warrant qualified professional or emergency support. | HealthyChildren.org / AAP; ReachOut Parents | The article explicitly distinguishes routine conversation from urgent safety action. |
| “Context, Change, and Cost” is an OWR observation framework. | Editorial synthesis from the checked guidance above. | Explicitly described as non-clinical and unable to reveal the teen’s mind. |

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No diagnosis of depression, anxiety, autism, trauma, or any other condition.
- No claim that introversion, quietness, bedroom time, or a low friend count proves loneliness.
- No promise that one conversation will resolve isolation or produce friendship.
- No invented testimonial, prevalence figure, or quantified outcome claim.
- No identification or quotation of community members.
