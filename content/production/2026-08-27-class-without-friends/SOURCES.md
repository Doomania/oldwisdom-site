## Material article claims: evidence and editorial labels

| Material claim or instruction | Label | Support and control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CDC’s definition of school connectedness includes care, value/support, and belonging at school. | **Official service info** | Directly drawn from the CDC page; wording kept close to the source. |
| School connectedness is associated with school and health outcomes. | **Official service info** | CDC describes associations and outcomes. ARTICLE uses “associates,” not causation, and explicitly says a class change does not guarantee belonging or an outcome. |
| School transitions can alter peer-relationship patterns; some students gain friendships or social standing. | **Evidence-aligned guidance** | Narrowly supported by Lorijn et al. (2024) and framed as variable, not predictive for an individual teen. |
| Pause → Map → Try → Escalate; calm observable conversation; map of classroom access; seven-school-day plan; scripts; end-of-week review. | **Editorial synthesis** | Original decision-support framework. It is practical parent guidance, not a validated intervention or a claim that it improves belonging. |
| A parent should contact the school with observable facts and a request for possible support rather than demand a placement result. | **Editorial ownership or escalation rule** | Deliberate OWR editorial boundary: preserves parent authority, respects school information, and avoids promising a timetable outcome. |
| If life is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or a crisis service immediately. | **Official service info** | WHO EMRO explicitly advises calling emergency services if life is in danger and provides country-specific crisis-hotline guidance. ARTICLE links the action at the exact sentence. |
| Ongoing distress or a functional impact can justify a school-support conversation; an access/support need may make a seating or other accommodation discussion useful. | **Evidence-aligned guidance** | NIMH advises seeking help when emotions/behaviour persist, cause distress, or interfere with functioning, and gives adjusted seating as one possible school accommodation. ARTICLE uses this to support a conversation, not to diagnose, demand a class change, or imply a global legal entitlement. |
| A safety concern, including threats, harassment, or being unable to take part safely, warrants prompt parent action with the school. | **Editorial ownership or escalation rule** | A narrow safety-first school-contact rule. It does not label ordinary exclusion as bullying, make a legal claim, or promise a school outcome. Immediate danger is separately linked to WHO at claim level. |

## Claims avoided and risk controls

| Avoided claim | Why avoided / control used |
| --- | --- |
| “A class change will help a teen belong, make friends, attend more, or learn better.” | No individual causal evidence supplied; ARTICLE explicitly says a move does not promise belonging or a particular outcome. |
| “Being away from friends is harmful” or “a teen must be put with friends.” | Transition experiences vary; the Lorijn study is used only for cautious variability, and the article asks for observable barriers first. |
| “A seven-day plan is evidence-based treatment.” | The plan is labelled editorial synthesis and framed as a practical trial, not therapy or a validated intervention. |
| “Distress or withdrawal means a mental-health condition.” | No diagnostic language. The article asks about observable functioning and refers immediate danger to emergency/crisis services. |
| “Schools must change a class placement.” | Avoided because policies and authority vary. The email asks what support is possible and does not direct an outcome. |
| Country-specific rights, timetabling rules, or emergency contacts. | Avoided to keep guidance globally usable and not imply legal or jurisdictional advice. |
