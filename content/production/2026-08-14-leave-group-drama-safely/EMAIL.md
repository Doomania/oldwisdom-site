## Email

Hi,

Your teen says they are done with the group.

Then someone posts a vague accusation. Another friend asks for screenshots. Your teen wants to explain everything publicly before everyone believes the wrong story.

This is where a parent can help without taking over the whole friendship.

Start by sorting the situation:

- **Ordinary disagreement:** one issue, no clear power imbalance, and a private repair may be possible.
- **Group drama:** gossip, screenshots, side-taking and repeated heat, without a present danger marker.
- **Safety bypass:** bullying or power imbalance, discriminatory harassment, threats, stalking, sextortion, doxxing, account compromise, non-consensual images, self-harm threats, violence, abuse or immediate danger.

For ordinary group drama, your teen can use a neutral participation boundary:

> “I’m not taking sides or sending screenshots. Leave me out of this one.”

That may remove some fresh fuel. It **cannot guarantee** that nobody will retaliate, gossip or exclude them.

When the situation crosses into danger, the roles change. The parent or another responsible adult owns safety, evidence support, account security, formal escalation and follow-up. The teen should not have to investigate, confront danger or keep a safety secret.

And no counter-attack: no pile-ons, leaked screenshots, burner accounts, threats or counter-gossip.

The full guide includes:

- the repair-or-exit choice;
- five exact exit and boundary scripts;
- how to choose one private ally without building a rival team;
- what evidence to preserve where safe;
- a quick account-security check;
- responsible school/platform/specialist escalation;
- a next-day recovery plan.

**Read the parent guide:** https://oldwisdomretold.com/articles/leave-group-drama-safely

A quiet exit is not magic armour. It is a way to protect what your family can control: safety, evidence, access, choices and tomorrow.
