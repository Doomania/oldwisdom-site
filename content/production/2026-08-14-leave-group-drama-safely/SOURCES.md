## Authoritative and empirical sources used

1. StopBullying.gov, “What Is Bullying.”  
   https://www.stopbullying.gov/bullying/what-is-bullying

   **Scope limit:** Supports distinguishing bullying from ordinary conflict by unwanted aggressive behaviour, a real or perceived power imbalance, and repetition or potential repetition. A rude exchange, exclusion or group split can hurt without establishing bullying.

2. StopBullying.gov, “Report Cyberbullying.”  
   https://www.stopbullying.gov/cyberbullying/how-to-report

   **Scope limit:** Supports not responding to or forwarding cyberbullying, preserving dates/times/descriptions and relevant messages, and reporting to platforms, schools or law enforcement as appropriate. The article narrows this further: preservation must be safe and private, and sexual or potentially illegal images must never be redistributed or downloaded merely as proof.

3. U.S. Department of Education, “Harassment, Bullying, and Retaliation.”  
   https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/civil-rights-laws/harassment-bullying-and-retaliation

   **Scope limit:** Supports a distinct civil-rights route for discriminatory harassment in US educational settings and attention to retaliation after a report. Legal duties and complaint routes differ by jurisdiction; the article tells readers to use applicable local routes.

4. Federal Trade Commission, “How To Recover Your Hacked Email or Social Media Account.”  
   https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recover-your-hacked-email-or-social-media-account

   **Scope limit:** Supports account recovery, security scans/updates, changing passwords, enabling MFA, checking altered settings and messages, and reviewing exposed information. It does not establish who compromised an account or guarantee future security.

5. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, “Turn On MFA.”  
   https://www.cisa.gov/secure-our-world/turn-mfa

   **Scope limit:** Supports MFA as an added security layer. The article presents MFA and related checks as risk reduction, not perfect protection.

6. National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, “Take It Down” and CyberTipline.  
   https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/  
   https://report.cybertip.org/

   **Scope limit:** Take It Down is a US-based free service intended to help remove or stop online sharing of nude, partially nude or sexually explicit images taken when the person was under 18. The service is designed so the image remains on the person’s device; the article therefore never tells a reader to send, forward or download an image as proof. CyberTipline is a US reporting route; use appropriate services elsewhere.

7. Federal Bureau of Investigation, “Sextortion.”  
   https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/scams-and-safety/common-frauds-and-scams/sextortion

   **Scope limit:** Supports treating sextortion as exploitation requiring adult and law-enforcement/specialist help rather than private bargaining or peer management. US reporting routes are location-specific.

8. Stalking Prevention, Awareness & Resource Center (SPARC), “Risk & Safety” and “Stalking Safety Strategies.”  
   https://www.stalkingawareness.org/risk-and-safety/  
   https://www.stalkingawareness.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Safety-Strategies.pdf

   **Scope limit:** Supports treating stalking as a safety-planning issue with unique risks, using documentation where safe, technology safety, and specialist help. It does not support confrontation or promise that documentation prevents escalation.

9. National Institute of Mental Health, “5 Action Steps to Help Someone Having Thoughts of Suicide.”  
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-to-help-someone-having-thoughts-of-suicide

   **Scope limit:** Supports asking directly, being present, helping keep the person safe, connecting capable support and following up. It does not make a minor a clinical risk assessor or sole responder. Service pathways differ by location.

10. 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, “Youth.”  
    https://988lifeline.org/help-yourself/youth/

    **Scope limit:** Supports reaching trusted adults and crisis support rather than keeping suicide or self-harm danger between peers. 988 is a US service; use local crisis or emergency services elsewhere.

11. Gaffney, Ttofi and Farrington et al. (2021), “Effectiveness of school-based programs to reduce bullying perpetration and victimization: An updated systematic review and meta-analysis.”  
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8356322/  
    https://doi.org/10.1002/cl2.1143

    **Design/claim limit:** The updated systematic review included 100 evaluations, including 88 newly identified studies, and found school programmes reduced bullying perpetration and victimisation on average. Programme-level average effects do not guarantee that one report, staff member or intervention will stop an individual case or prevent retaliation.

## Claims and promises intentionally avoided

- No promise that leaving quietly prevents retaliation, gossip, exclusion or reputation damage.
- No claim that ignoring people makes them lose interest.
- No claim that blocking ends a problem or that one ally protects reputation.
- No claim that one report guarantees school, platform or police action.
- No instruction to remain exposed in order to collect evidence.
- No redistribution, forwarding or downloading of sexual or potentially illegal images.
- No retaliatory pile-ons, leaks, burner accounts, threats, counter-gossip, public takedowns or amateur investigations.
- No parent impersonation, forced confrontation or blanket surveillance.
- No collapsing one rude exchange or exclusion into bullying without the defining power/repetition elements.
- No presenting the Low-Friction Exit as validated, clinical, legal or universally effective.
