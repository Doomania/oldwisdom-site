## Directly inspected and used

### S1. Raine, Zimmer-Gembeck, Skinner, Scott, and Hawes, “Parental Support and Adolescents’ Coping with Academic Stressors: A Longitudinal Study of Parents’ Influence Beyond Academic Pressure and Achievement”

- **Organisation/publication:** Journal of Youth and Adolescence; full text in PubMed Central
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10522509/
- **Directly inspected:** Yes, full article HTML.
- **Design/population:** Two-wave, one-year longitudinal survey; 839 Australian students in grades 5–10, mean age 12.2, 96% aged 10–15.
- **Claim-safe use:** Adolescents reporting more parental support also reported more engagement coping and comfort-seeking; negative parent interactions were associated with disengagement coping. Longitudinal models linked support with changes in reported coping.
- **Limits:** Convenience sample; adolescent self-report; two waves; pandemic timing/format confounding; authors say repeated or experimental research is needed to clarify direction and process. It did not test FEEL → FACTS → ONE CHANGE or prove a parent script improves grades.

### S2. Çelik, “Academic motivation in adolescents: the role of parental autonomy support, psychological needs satisfaction and self-control”

- **Organisation/publication:** Frontiers in Psychology; full text in PubMed Central
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11116802/
- **Directly inspected:** Yes, full article HTML.
- **Design/population:** Cross-sectional, online convenience sample; 427 Turkish high-school students aged 14–19; self-report measures and structural equation modelling.
- **Claim-safe use:** Perceived parental autonomy support was indirectly associated with academic motivation through reported need satisfaction and self-control.
- **Limits:** Cross-sectional design prevents causal inference; self-report; one cultural context; no parent response to a failed test was tested. Used only as modest background for offering a real choice within clear expectations.

### S3. Education Endowment Foundation, “Metacognition and self-regulation”

- **Organisation:** Education Endowment Foundation, UK
- **URL:** https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation?utm_content_text=null
- **Directly inspected:** Yes, full rendered page after the bare URL returned a bot challenge.
- **Evidence base:** EEF Teaching and Learning Toolkit synthesis; 355 studies; review updated May 2025; evidence security rated high, with a noted loss of one padlock because many studies were not independently evaluated.
- **Claim-safe use:** These educational approaches explicitly teach pupils to plan, monitor, and evaluate learning strategies. EEF says context and professional judgement matter.
- **Limits:** Teacher/classroom evidence, not a trial of this article, family script, or any single study method. No grade-improvement estimate is repeated in the article.

### S5. National Institute of Mental Health, “Children and Mental Health: Is This Just a Stage?”
