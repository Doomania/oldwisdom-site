## Email

Hi,

Your teen has just seen the photos. Nearly everyone was there. They were not invited.

You want to protect them—perhaps by ringing another parent, calling the group cruel or announcing that those friendships are over.

Before you call anyone, try:

> “I can see this hurt. I’m not going to ring anyone or tell you what the friendship means before I understand it. Do you want listening, help sorting the facts, or options?”

Then use **HOLD → SORT → CHOOSE → WIDEN**:

1. **HOLD** — let the loss matter before trying to fix it.
2. **SORT** — separate “I was not invited this time” from “nobody wants me.” Check whether this is one event, a friendship shift, possible bullying or a safety concern.
3. **CHOOSE** — offer safe options: wait, ask one person privately, name the hurt, or watch whether effort is returned.
4. **WIDEN** — make the week bigger than one group without launching a replacement-friend project.

A useful fact-versus-story line is:

> “The fact is that you were not invited this time. The story your mind is offering is that nobody wants you. That story may feel true tonight, but we do not know that from one event.”

[Repeated deliberate harm with a power imbalance](https://www.education.govt.nz/parents-and-caregivers/schools-year-0-13/health-safety-and-wellbeing/bullying-help-and-support-your-child) needs adult and school action. [Immediate danger](https://www.police.govt.nz/faq/when-should-i-call-111?nondesktop) or [suicide/self-harm risk](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-for-helping-someone-in-emotional-pain) bypasses the ordinary framework and needs urgent local emergency or crisis support.

**Read the parent guide:** https://oldwisdomretold.com/articles/teen-left-out-by-friends

You do not have to stand back from safety. You also do not have to stand in the centre of an ordinary friendship decision.

Eric
