## Directly inspected and authoritative sources

1. **New Zealand Ministry of Education, “Bullying: help and support for your child,” updated 4 May 2026.**
   https://www.education.govt.nz/parents-and-caregivers/schools-year-0-13/health-safety-and-wellbeing/bullying-help-and-support-your-child

   **Claim-safe use:** Bullying is deliberately harmful, involves a power imbalance and is usually repeated; it differs from one-off conflict or unintentional harm. Supports school responsibility, reporting routes, support and follow-up. New Zealand institutional details are not presented as universal law or policy.

2. **Nemours KidsHealth, “Rejection and How to Handle It.”**
   https://kidshealth.org/en/teens/rejection.html

   **Claim-safe use:** Rejection hurts; feelings can be acknowledged and put into words; facts can be separated from imagined explanations and global self-judgments. Teen-facing educational guidance only. It does not validate OWR’s framework or show that one conversation changes peer outcomes.

3. **New Zealand Police, “When should I call 111?” directly inspected 14 August 2026.**
   https://www.police.govt.nz/faq/when-should-i-call-111?nondesktop

   **Claim-safe use:** Supports emergency action when someone is badly injured, in danger or at serious risk. The article universalises the action to “contact local emergency services” and does not publish New Zealand’s number as universal. Ordinary exclusion does not meet this threshold.

4. **US National Institute of Mental Health, “5 Action Steps to Help Someone Having Thoughts of Suicide,” revised 2024.**
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-for-helping-someone-in-emotional-pain

   **Claim-safe use:** Supports asking directly about suicide, listening, helping keep the person safe, connecting them with crisis/community resources and capable support, and following up. The US-only number is not presented as universal. This source does not support bullying or violence branches.

5. **Shah, Szwedo and Allen, longitudinal multi-method observational study, N=184, adolescence to age 22.**
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9837864/

   **Claim-safe use:** Some autonomy-restricting measures at age 13 predicted selected parent-reported dependency outcomes at age 22 in this sample; findings differed by measure, parent and age. Supports only a broad developmental direction toward age-appropriate ownership. It does not test exclusion, parent calls, HOLD → SORT → CHOOSE → WIDEN or causation.

6. **Teuber et al., five-year longitudinal study, N=789 German students aged 10–17.**
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9135772/

   **Claim-safe use:** Supportive parenting combined warmth and autonomy support; controlling profiles were associated with poorer academic and psychological development. Population, profile and observational limits are preserved. It does not validate a script or predict an individual teen’s outcome.

## Editorial safeguarding floor

**Do not ask the teen to confront a group, retaliate online, investigate or gather more proof while exposed.** This is conservative OWR editorial guidance. It is not claimed as a rule or effect established by the cited authorities.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim about why the group excluded the teen.
- No diagnosis, mind-reading or label such as “toxic,” “jealous” or “narcissistic.”
- No claim that all exclusion is bullying or that bullying must repeat before immediate help.
- No claim that parent contact always backfires.
- No claim that this guidance prevents loneliness, depression, dependency or future rejection.
- No demand for an invitation, forced confrontation, immediate forgiveness, reconciliation or replacement friends.
- No claim that reporting guarantees a fast, fair or retaliation-free outcome.
- No claim that *The Social Playbook* can secure inclusion or replace adult safety action.
