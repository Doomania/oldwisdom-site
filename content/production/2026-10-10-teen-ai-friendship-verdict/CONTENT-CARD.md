# Content card — AI verdict on a teen friendship

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Audience:** Busy parents of teens 12–17 (parent only)
**Status:** READY_FOR_DRAFT — independent concept QA PASS (2026-10-10); article and publication QA still pending
**Slug:** teen-ai-friendship-verdict
**Test angle:** A chatbot called my teen's friend manipulative; what should I say?

## Reader job
Respond to an AI friendship verdict without endorsing a label, ridiculing the teen's use, demanding private chats, or taking over an ordinary friend decision.

## Promise
Three questions to examine an AI answer, protect personal details, and choose a next real-world move with the teen.

## Observable moment
At the kitchen table your teen says (illustrative scenario, not a sourced quotation), “I pasted our messages into AI and it says my friend is manipulating me.”

## Belief shift
- **From:** I have to prove the bot wrong, ban the tool, or accept its verdict to protect my kid.
- **To:** I can take the feeling seriously without treating AI's label as evidence, ask what information it had and lacked, and keep real-life safety and teen agency separate.

## Mechanism
**AI ANSWER CHECK** (unvalidated OWR editorial framework): “What were you hoping to learn?” → “What did the tool see, and what couldn't it know?” → “What private details can we stop sharing, and what one real-world step do you want to take?” Review the *answer*, not score the friend. Parent retains safety and household/device boundaries; teen owns an ordinary friendship decision.

## Required lanes
- Ordinary ambiguity: a chatbot answer is not a diagnosis or proof of intent; teen can talk to friend, pause, or decide no action after checking context.
- Privacy/access: do not paste more of anyone's identifiable texts, images, names or location; check the specific platform's settings together. Keep accessible communication and relevant disability support; avoid treating a teen's preferred written channel as itself a problem.
- Online mistreatment, including a serious single threat or privacy breach: listen, understand context, and consider school/platform response supported by UNICEF cyberbullying guidance; do not classify an ordinary awkward exchange as bullying or wait for serious harm to repeat.
- Wellbeing/functioning: NIMH supports seeking health-professional advice if emotions or behaviour last weeks, cause distress, **or** interfere with school, home, or friends. UNICEF AI guidance supports gentle check-ins about sleep, friends, and other activity; no AI diagnosis or invented threshold.
- Immediate danger: seek local emergency help now as a precautionary editorial safety route, not a WHO friendship-threat protocol; do not wait for AI or the guide.
