# Evidence map — presentation reps parent plan

**Status:** DRAFT_ONLY. Direct source inspection recorded 2026-09-23.

## Authoritative sources

1. **UK National Careers Service — [Interview tips](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/interview-advice)** (government service; directly inspected).
   - Says to think about what an interviewer might ask, practise answers to common questions, and ask for a question to be repeated or explained if needed.
   - **Claim-safe use:** a teen may choose one likely-question rehearsal with a trusted person; an interview reset line can request clarification.
   - **Limit:** career-interview guidance, not adolescent or school-performance research; it does not test out-loud practice or predict selection.
2. **UK National Careers Service — [How to answer common interview questions](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/top-10-interview-questions)** (government service; directly inspected).
   - Invites readers to practise responses to common questions.
   - **Claim-safe use:** preparation can include one likely-question rehearsal.
   - **Limit:** not evidence that rehearsing causes confidence, lower anxiety, a better grade, or a job/tryout result.
3. **NIMH — [Children and Mental Health: Is This Just a Stage?](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/children-and-mental-health)** (U.S. public-health authority; directly inspected).
   - Says to consider help when emotions/behaviour last weeks, cause distress, or interfere at school, home, or with friends; seek immediate help for unsafe behaviour or talk of hurting self/others.
   - **Claim-safe use:** route persistent/interfering distress or immediate danger outside this ordinary-preparation guide.
   - **Limit:** not a diagnostic checklist; ordinary nerves before one event do not establish a condition.

## Editorial mechanism
**Three-Reps Plan** is OWR editorial synthesis—not a tested intervention:
1. one out-loud run-through;
2. one likely-question rehearsal; and
3. one reset line.

A parent offers a bounded audience or one question; the teen chooses whether to practise and remains responsible for content and participation.

## Claim controls
- Do not promise confidence, calm, selection, a grade, a successful performance, or reduced anxiety.
- Do not use mental-health language for ordinary nerves.
- Treat performance-specific scripts as examples, not guarantees.

- `teen-wont-ask-teacher-for-help`: contacting a teacher about a specific academic block.
- This guide: preparation for one imminent evaluative speaking/performance moment.
