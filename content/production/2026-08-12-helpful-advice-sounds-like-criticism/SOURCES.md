## Authoritative sources used in the article

1. Newman, M. & Davis, E. L., “A Helping Hand Isn’t Always So Helpful: Parental Autonomy Support Moderates the Effectiveness of Interpersonal Emotion Regulation for Emerging Adults.” *Emerging Adulthood* (online 2023 / issue 2024).
   https://doi.org/10.1177/21676968231222304

   **Sample/design limit retained:** 194 emerging adults aged 18–25, primarily college students, retrospectively reporting one parental interaction. Participant-report, correlational design; not an experiment with younger teenagers. It cannot establish that unsolicited advice causes harm, that autonomy-supportive communication causes advice to be received well, or that NEW → NECESSARY → THEIRS improves outcomes.

2. Tanaka et al., “Longitudinal Association between Maternal Autonomy Support and Controlling Parenting and Adolescents’ Depressive Symptoms.”
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9851735/

   **Sample/design limit retained:** Two-wave questionnaire study of 408 Japanese mother–adolescent pairs, four months apart. Mothers only; nonexperimental; low-to-moderate reporter agreement does not establish which account was correct in a particular exchange. The article does not use the depressive-symptom analysis to make a claim about ordinary advice.

3. Kobak et al., “Adapting to the Changing Needs of Adolescents: Parenting Practices and Challenges to Sensitive Attunement.”
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5886742/

   **Sample/design limit retained:** Narrative developmental review. It supports the compatibility of engagement, supervision/guidance, open communication, and increasing autonomy, but does not isolate advice wording, test the OWR framework, or establish that one communication move causes better outcomes.

4. American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren, “How to Communicate With and Listen to Your Teen: 3 Key Tips,” updated 7 August 2024.
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Communicate-with-a-Teenager.aspx

   **Authority/design limit retained:** Expert pediatric guidance, not an intervention trial. It supports parents sharing values and opinions, avoiding judgmental or condescending lectures, and using concrete guidance during distress. It does not show that concise wording prevents shutdown, prohibit unsolicited advice, or remove rules, correction, supervision, direct intervention, or consequences.

5. American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren, “What to Expect When You Call 911,” updated 4 September 2024.
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Calling-911-How-and-When-to-Call-for-Help.aspx

   **Authority/scope limit retained:** US pediatric emergency guidance supporting emergency medical help for severe illness or injury. The article translates the action globally as contacting local emergency services and does not present US numbers as universal.

6. World Health Organization, “Suicide,” questions and answers, 18 September 2024.
   https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/suicide

   **Authority/scope limit retained:** WHO advises contacting emergency services or a crisis line when someone is in immediate danger of self-harm. The article does not turn ordinary disagreement, defensiveness, or one-off rudeness into a self-harm or emergency signal.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim that unsolicited advice is inherently harmful or always unwelcome.
- No claim that a teenager’s dislike of feedback makes the advice wrong, unfair, or unnecessary.
- No claim that advice, correction, or one difficult exchange is psychological control, abuse, or a cause of poor mental health.
- No diagnosis of anxiety, defiance, trauma, neurodivergence, avoidance, overload, or family dysfunction from “I know,” silence, defensiveness, or disagreement.
- No causal claim from the Newman/Davis, Tanaka, or review evidence.
- No claim that NEW → NECESSARY → THEIRS has been experimentally validated or will prevent shutdown.
- No promise that concise advice, autonomy support, or returned ownership will produce agreement, disclosure, compliance, or better adjustment.
- No claim that parents must wait for permission before giving guidance, enforcing a boundary, meeting legal/medical duties, or acting for safety.
- No claim that independence means removing repetition, scaffolding, disability support, learning support, supervision, or proportionate consequences.
- No use of community language as prevalence evidence, factual authority, or proof of consensus.
