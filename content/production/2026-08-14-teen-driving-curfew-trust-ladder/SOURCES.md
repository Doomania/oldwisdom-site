## Authoritative and empirical sources

1. CDC, “Reducing Risk for Teen Drivers” (updated 4 Aug 2025).
   https://www.cdc.gov/teen-drivers/prevention/index.html
   **Safe use:** Novice-driver risk, graduated exposure, and the 14-study GDL meta-analysis associated with about 19% fewer injury crashes and 21% fewer fatal crashes among 16-year-olds. **Boundary:** policy-system association, not proof for a household curfew or TRUST.

2. CDC, “Eight Danger Zones” and “Parent-Teen Driving Agreement” (updated 2025).
   https://www.cdc.gov/teen-drivers/parents-are-the-key/danger-zones.html
   https://www.cdc.gov/teen-drivers/parents-are-the-key/driving-agreement.html
   **Safe use:** Inexperience, passengers, night driving, distraction, fatigue and impairment are recognised risk areas; written expectations can be reviewed as experience grows. **Boundary:** guidance is not efficacy proof.

3. Fell, Todd & Voas (2011), US state-panel FARS policy study.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3251518/
   **Safe use:** Nighttime restrictions were associated with an estimated 10% reduction in 16–17-year-old driver involvement in nighttime fatal crashes. **Boundary:** observational policy evidence, not individual causation or a universal household hour.

4. Simons-Morton et al. (2006), Checkpoints randomized trial; same 3,743 Connecticut families at two follow-ups.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16469334/
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16620739/
   **Safe use:** The intervention increased parent limits and later showed some self-reported risky-driving/violation differences. **Boundary:** no crash effect; follow-ups are not independent samples.

5. Hamann et al. (2014), cross-sectional survey of 163 predominantly White Iowa parent-teen dyads.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8764704/
   **Safe use:** Parent and teen expectations showed low agreement across several driving conditions and return times. **Boundary:** expectations, not behaviour or crash causation.

6. NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi / Drive, current restricted-licence conditions.
   https://drive.govt.nz/learner-licence/road-code/the-basics-of-safety/the-driver-licence/restricted-licence-conditions
   **Verified current wording:** NZ restricted drivers are not allowed to drive without a supervisor between 10pm and 5am. **Boundary:** NZ example only; readers must check their current jurisdiction and licence stage.

7. NZTA breakdown guidance; US NWS flood safety; MedlinePlus emergency recognition; American Red Cross emergency-action guidance.
   https://nzta.govt.nz/roadcode/general-road-code/about-driver-responsibility/breakdowns
   https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-turn-around-dont-drown
   https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001927.htm
   https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/articles/the-three-cs-of-first-aid-check-call-care
   **Safe use:** Stop/seek appropriate help rather than force an unsafe drive. **Boundary:** detailed roadside, medical, emergency and reporting actions remain location- and situation-specific.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No universal correct curfew hour.
- No claim that TRUST is validated or guarantees crash prevention.
- No claim that grades, chores, character, GPS, or a clean record prove driving readiness.
- No claim that full licensure removes nighttime or novice-driver risk.
- No implication that safe pickup erases later accountability.
- No country-specific law or emergency process presented as global.
