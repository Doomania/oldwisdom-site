## Sources

- **S1:** Edutopia, [Group Work That Works](https://www.edutopia.org/article/group-work-works/) — classroom-practice context only.
- **S2:** National Institute of Mental Health, [Children and Mental Health: Is This Just a Stage?](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/children-and-mental-health) — persistent distress/interference and urgent-safety boundary; US contacts are not universal.

## Editorial devices not tested by sources

- Contribution Ledger and its six fields;
- all teen, parent, and teacher scripts;
- suggested timing and escalation thresholds;
- the factual-message format; and
- the ordinary/access/wellbeing/safety routing structure.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- A ledger, neutral request, or parent script will make classmates contribute.
- A missing contribution proves laziness, malice, bullying, a disability, or a mental health condition.
- A teacher will redistribute work, adjust marks, or resolve the issue in a particular way.
- Parents should contact classmates, do the assignment, or collect evidence beyond the task facts.
- A single group-project dispute establishes a wellbeing condition.
- US school, health, or crisis routes apply worldwide.

## Residual claim risk

**Low to moderate, controlled.** This guide translates limited classroom-practice context and an official wellbeing boundary into an untested household tool. The article labels the mechanism editorial, limits it to observable work, makes no fairness or outcome promise, and routes access, persistent distress, and urgent safety away from peer problem-solving.
