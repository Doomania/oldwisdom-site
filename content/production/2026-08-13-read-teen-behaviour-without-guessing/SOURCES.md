# Sources and Claim Audit

Research date: 2026-08-13  
Audience: PARENT  
Status: review bundle; no publication action performed

## Source map

1. NIMH, “Children and Mental Health: Is This Just a Stage?”  
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/children-and-mental-health

   **Supports:** concern based on duration, distress and disruption across home/school/friends; older-child warning signs; immediate action for unsafe behaviour or harm language; assessment using multiple contexts and history.  
   **Limit:** no diagnosis from one behaviour or article checklist; unsafe behaviour does not require waiting weeks.

2. CDC, “Essentials for Parenting Teens,” “Tips for Understanding and Adapting,” and “Tips for Coaching Emotions.”  
   https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-teens/about/index.html  
   https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-teens/tips-understanding-and-adapting/index.html  
   https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-teens/tips-coaching-emotions/index.html

   **Supports:** adolescence involves physical/emotional change; calm, empathetic modelling, communication, independence and safe relationships are useful foundations.  
   **Limit:** broad guidance does not explain one teen’s motive or validate READ as a tested intervention.

3. AAP / HealthyChildren, teen mental-health warning signs and communication guidance.  
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/teen/Pages/Mental-Health-and-Teens-Watch-for-Danger-Signs.aspx  
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Communicate-with-a-Teenager.aspx

   **Supports:** sustained changes in everyday patterns and function deserve attention; listening without instant judgment/advice supports communication.  
   **Limit:** warning signs prompt further assessment; they do not prove a disorder.

4. AACAP, “When to Seek Help for Your Child.”  
   https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/When-To-Seek-Help-For-Your-Child-024.aspx

   **Supports:** persistent problems, functional disruption and concern shared by others can justify qualified consultation.  
   **Limit:** no single household behaviour is diagnostic.

5. NIMH, “Frequently Asked Questions About Suicide.”  
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/suicide-faq

   **Supports:** asking directly does not create suicidal thoughts; do not leave a person alone when there is suicidal intent; use urgent crisis or emergency help.  
   **Limit:** does not make a parent a clinical risk assessor; service details must be localised.

6. NHS, “First aid” and “Poisoning.”  
   https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/first-aid/  
   https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/

   **Supports:** immediate medical escalation for severe injury, breathing danger, unresponsiveness, or poisoning/overdose.  
   **Limit:** UK pathways are location-specific; parents should use local emergency services.

7. NSPCC, “What to do if a child reveals abuse.”  
   https://www.nspcc.org.uk/keeping-children-safe/reporting-abuse/what-to-do-child-reveals-abuse/

   **Supports:** calm listening, not forcing details, explaining next steps, and reporting safeguarding concerns.  
   **Limit:** UK reporting pathways are location-specific; parents should not investigate or confront an alleged abuser.

## Claim audit

| Article move | Evidence basis | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Separate observation from interpretation | Editorial decision aid consistent with multi-context assessment | Does not claim motive can always be known |
| Examine change, setting, repetition, recovery and cost | NIMH/AAP duration, distress, functioning, baseline and context guidance | Not a validated screening score |
| Ask one small question and listen | CDC/AAP communication guidance | No promise of disclosure |
| Connect / coach / boundary / get help | Editorial synthesis of parenting and escalation guidance | Options can combine; understanding never excuses harm |
| Seek help for sustained/worsening broad change | NIMH/AAP/AACAP | Warning signs are not diagnoses |
| Immediate bypass for suicide/harm language | NIMH suicide FAQ | Uses local services; does not make parent/teen a clinical assessor |
| Immediate medical danger or overdose | NHS first-aid and poisoning guidance | Uses local services; parent is not assigned treatment responsibility |
| Abuse disclosure or selective fear | NSPCC safeguarding guidance | Listen and report responsibly; do not investigate or confront |

## Claims intentionally avoided

- All behaviour is communication.
- Defiance is always distress.
- Privacy proves danger or depression.
- Irritability is “just hormones.”
- A parent can infer manipulation from one behaviour.
- READ identifies a diagnosis, guarantees disclosure, prevents harm, or improves outcomes.
