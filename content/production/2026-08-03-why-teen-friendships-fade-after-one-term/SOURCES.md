## Brief claim audit

| Content claim used in batch | Support | Wording guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Friendship drift often increases when school structure changes, including transitions between terms, classes, and school stages. | Lessard and Juvonen; Ferguson et al.; Torres et al. | Framed as a common pattern in adolescent friendship research, not as an absolute rule for every teen. |
| Maintaining some friendships across changing school contexts is associated with stronger school belonging or school-related adjustment. | Lessard and Juvonen; Ferguson et al.; CDC school connectedness page. | Presented as a directional finding about belonging and adjustment, not as a guarantee of better outcomes from any one friendship. |
| Shared settings such as classes, activities, and peer groups help sustain friendships, which is one reason they can weaken when those structures disappear. | Torres et al.; Bohnert et al. | Used to explain why drift can happen without conflict; not used to imply teens are powerless without institutional structure. |
| Reciprocity, shared friends, and maintained contact make friendship persistence more likely. | Torres et al.; Ferguson et al.; Rousseau et al. | The batch turns this into practical coaching advice without claiming a formula that always preserves a friendship. |
| Compatibility and similarity matter for whether friendships endure. | Hartl et al. | Used to normalize some drift and avoid over-personalizing every fade as failure. |
| The "second-conversation rule" and "friendship carry-over plan" are editorial frameworks built from the evidence above. | Inference from maintenance, reciprocity, and school-transition findings across the source set. | Explicitly treated as practical coaching tools, not direct quoted research terms. |

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim that friendship drift means a teen has a mental health disorder or clinical condition.
- No diagnosis of anxiety, autism, ADHD, depression, or trauma.
- No promise that one follow-up message will create friendship, popularity, or belonging.
- No invented testimonials, case studies, or quantified outcome promises.
- No long quotations from copyrighted research sources.

## Review notes for human editor

- If the title is localized for U.S. audiences, "term" may be swapped for "semester" or "school term" in packaging while preserving the queue topic in source files.
- Keep the skills-reflection CTA accurate: it is informational, not a diagnostic or clinical assessment, and not a required route to a purchase.
