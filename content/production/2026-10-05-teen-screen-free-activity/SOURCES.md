# Sources — Teen-Chosen Screen-Free Shared Leisure

Reviewed 2026-10-05. Source links appear beside the article's claims. Scripts, scenarios, and the optional one-time invitation are editorial suggestions, **not tested interventions**. No claim here says reducing screen minutes causes better family relationships.

## Claim-level evidence

### S1 — Pew Research Center, “How Teens and Parents Approach Screen Time” (March 11, 2024)
- URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/03/11/how-teens-and-parents-approach-screen-time/
- Method: online survey September 26–October 23, 2023, of 1,453 US teens ages 13–17 and their parents, weighted for each population.
- Supports: 36% of teens reported cutting back on phone use and 63% had not; 72% at least sometimes felt peaceful and 44% anxious without a smartphone; 46% of teens reported a parent at least sometimes distracted by their phone during a conversation; 38% of teens and 38% of parents reported at least sometimes arguing about teen phone time.
- Boundary: cross-sectional self-reports, not an activity trial or proof that an invitation will change behavior. Peaceful and anxious categories can overlap; percentages do not describe the same individual's response to a proposed activity.

### S2 — Moreno et al., “Effect of a Family Media Use Plan on Media Rule Engagement Among Adolescents” (JAMA Pediatrics, 2021)
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7835954/
- Supports: randomized trial of 1,520 English-speaking parent–adolescent pairs, ages 12–17; intervention completion did not significantly change teen-reported media-rule engagement at approximately two months (between-group difference −0.1, 95% CI −1.1 to 0.9). Nearly half did not complete follow-up.
- Boundary: tests the AAP family media plan, **not** screen-free leisure invitations; high attrition and intervention/outcome specificity preclude transferring a null result to activity choice.

### S3 — American Academy of Pediatrics, “How to Make a Family Media Plan” (updated January 2026)
- URL: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/How-to-Make-a-Family-Media-Use-Plan.aspx
- Supports: collaboratively tailored family plan, adult role modeling, alternate activities, and reassessment as family needs change.
- Boundary: professional recommendations, not an efficacy trial. The article does not claim a family plan compels a teen to join an activity.

### S4 — American Academy of Pediatrics, “A Child-Friendly Digital World: AAP Releases New Media Recommendations” (2026)
- URL: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/creating-a-child-friendly-digital-world-AAP-releases-new-media-recommendations.aspx
- Supports: parents cannot carry the digital-wellbeing burden alone; community spaces and broader platform/environment action matter.
- Boundary: recommendation, not evidence that a particular third space is available, affordable, or safe to a given family.

### S5 — Anschütz, “Extraordinary everydayness” (Global Networks, 2022)
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9313570/
- Supports: 18-month ethnography with 25 young people of Ghanaian background, ages 14–25, in Belgium and Ghana; online ties with origin-country peers can form part of meaningful connections before and across visits.
- Boundary: qualitative, context-specific experience. Not representative of all migrants, all Ghanaian families, or teens generally. The vignette about protecting a family call is an editorial example, not an outcome from this study.
