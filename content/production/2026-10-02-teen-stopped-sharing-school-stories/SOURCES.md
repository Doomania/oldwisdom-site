# Sources — When Your Teen Stops Telling You About School

**Source review:** 2026-10-03 (NZ release context). AAP, WHO, and both NIMH primary pages were reachable and checked. The Raising Children Network page could not be verified at text level, so its citation was removed from the article. The article's scripts, examples, and four-step recap are editorial tools, not tested interventions.

## Claim-level evidence

### S1 — American Academy of Pediatrics, “How to Communicate With and Listen to Your Teen: 3 Key Tips”
- URL: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Communicate-with-a-Teenager.aspx
- Supports: listen without an alarmed reaction, avoid catastrophising and lecturing when a teen raises a concern (article, “When a feeling slips out”).
- Boundary: clinical/parenting guidance, not evidence that any script restores sharing or that quiet means a parent made a mistake.


### S3 — World Health Organization, “Responding to children and adolescents who have been sexually abused”
- URL: https://www.who.int/news/item/19-10-2017-responding-to-children-and-adolescents-who-have-been-sexually-abused
- Supports: for a possible abuse disclosure, prioritise safety, privacy, choice and autonomy; explain limits of confidentiality and reporting obligations (article, “Check before you pass their story on”).
- Boundary: guidance primarily for frontline health workers responding to possible sexual abuse, not a diagnostic framework for ordinary school-story privacy. The different-safe-adult advice is a safety-conscious editorial application, not a verbatim WHO protocol or jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

### S4 — National Institute of Mental Health, “Children and Mental Health: Is This Just a Stage?”
- URL: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/children-and-mental-health
- Supports: consider help when emotions or behaviour last for weeks, cause distress, or impair functioning; seek immediate help for unsafe behaviour or talk of harm to self or others (article, “Know when listening alone is not enough”).
- Boundary: diminished school stories alone do not establish a mental-health condition. US service numbers are not generalised internationally; article points to local help.

### S5 — National Institute of Mental Health, “Frequently Asked Questions About Suicide”
- URL: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/suicide-faq
- Supports: urgent action and staying with a person when safe if they intend to end their life (article, urgent-safety paragraph).
- Boundary: crisis guidance, not a substitute for emergency services or an individual risk assessment.
