## Editorial framework note

The article references Self-Determination Theory (autonomy/competence/relatedness) as an explanatory framework for understanding youth sport engagement. This is presented as an editorial interpretation of established psychological concepts applied to sport contexts, not as a citation of specific research findings. Scholarly access to youth sport SDT literature may be limited by paywalls or CAPTCHA barriers, so the article uses the framework conceptually rather than claiming to cite specific inaccessible studies.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim that arriving-playing-leaving proves social anxiety, exclusion, bullying, or low confidence.
- No claim that all teens need close friendships on their sports teams.
- No promise that suggested connection points will lead to friendship or social acceptance.
- No instruction for parents to arrange friendships, confront teammates, or override teen's stated preferences.
- No diagnostic language about shyness, social skills deficits, or mental health conditions.
- No claim that coaches are responsible for creating friendships or fixing social dynamics.
- No universal timeline for when to contact coach or step back.
- No use of sport participation advice as substitute for urgent safety action or qualified support where NIMH thresholds are met.
