# If an AI Fake Nude of Your Teen Is Shared: Start Here

Your teen shows you something on their phone. Or you hear about it from another parent. Someone has made or shared a sexual image that looks like your child. It may not be a real photo. It may be AI-made. It can still feel serious.

You do not need to solve everything at the kitchen table. Start smaller.

## Say this first

> “You are not in trouble for telling me. We will handle this with you.”

Then stop talking for a moment.

A short pause can help you slow the first conversation.

Avoid promising that the image will disappear or that you know exactly what happens next. You may not. Try this instead:

> “I do not have every answer yet. But you do not have to handle this alone.”

## The five calm moves

### 1. Connect before you collect facts

Your first job is not to build a case. It is to stay on your teen’s side.

Try:

> “Thank you for telling me.”
> “This is not your fault.”
> “We can take one step at a time.”

You can ask for the basics later. Right now, avoid turning the first conversation into a cross-examination.

### 2. Take It Down: read the live requirements

For **Take It Down**, the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children says not to send, share, or download material in order to use the service. It creates a hash—a digital match—on the device rather than requiring an upload.

Check the live service requirements before relying on it.

### 3. Use verified help

This guide cannot decide which service is right for your family. It does not replace current, verified support for your location and situation.

The National Center for Missing & Exploited Children explains that AI can be used to create sexualised images of children, including by classmates, and links to reporting and removal resources. That does not mean every situation has the same answer. It means you do not need to invent one alone.

### 4. Check the removal option carefully

If a sexual image or video of a person was taken before they were 18, **Take It Down** may be relevant. The service says it can create a hash from certain images or videos on the device. NCMEC also says the tool can help when real or AI-generated explicit images are circulating.

Two important limits:

- check the live service requirements; and
- do not expect a guarantee that an image will vanish everywhere.

This is one option, not a promise and not a full plan for every situation.

### 5. Make a calm follow-up time

The first conversation may be short. That is okay.

You might say:

> “Let’s take a break. I will read the verified options, and we will talk again after dinner.”

A planned second conversation gives you permission to pause before deciding what comes next.

## What not to make this mean

An AI-made image may feel upsetting. But you do not need to decide, in one evening, what it means about your teen, another young person, their friendships, or their future.

Avoid these shortcuts:

- “Why did you let this happen?”
- “It is only fake, so ignore it.”
- “I will make sure everyone knows who did this.”
- “I promise I can get rid of it tonight.”

Replace them with one steadier question:

> “What would help you feel less alone while we work out the next step?”

That is not a promise to hand over every decision. It is a way to stay connected while you use the right verified support.

## A small note about the scale of the problem

In a 2026 US survey of 1,314 teens, nearly half said they had seen AI-generated explicit material. Among those teens, one in four said it showed themselves or someone they knew. That survey does not tell us what is happening in every family or place. It is one reason this topic is worth talking about calmly.

## Keep the next step small

Tonight, you do not need a perfect speech. You need a safe first line, a clear reading of Take It Down’s live requirements if relevant, and one verified source of help.

Start here:

1. Say: “You are not in trouble for telling me.”
2. Read Take It Down’s live requirements if you are considering that option.
3. Use a current, verified support source for your situation.
4. Set a time for the next calm conversation.

Your teen does not need you to be an internet investigator. They need you to be the adult who did not turn away.
