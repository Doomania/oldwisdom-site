## Authoritative sources used

1. Weymouth et al. (2016), “A Meta-Analysis of Parent–Adolescent Conflict: Disagreement, Hostility, and Youth Maladjustment.”
   https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jftr.12126

   **Design/claim limit:** Meta-analysis of 401 effects from 52 studies in a largely observational literature. It supports distinguishing disagreement, hostility, and composite conflict measures and reports associations with youth maladjustment. It does not establish causation, equate ordinary disagreement with hostility, or show that VOICE → HEAR → CARRY improves outcomes.

2. Van Doorn, Branje and Meeus (2011), “Developmental Changes in Conflict Resolution Styles in Parent–Adolescent Relationships.”
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3003790/

   **Design/claim limit:** Four-wave self-report study of 314 Dutch early adolescents and both parents. Positive problem-solving, conflict engagement, and withdrawal changed across adolescence, with different mother/father patterns. It does not establish that a specific script causes development or predicts one family’s result.

3. Moed et al. (2015), “Parent–Adolescent Conflict as Sequences of Reciprocal Negative Emotion.”
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7992108/

   **Design/claim limit:** Observed six-minute conflict discussions in 138 parent–adolescent dyads, with a largely White US sample and mean adolescent age of 13.44. Prolonged reciprocal negative emotion was associated with unresolved perceptions and behaviour-problem measures. The design does not prove causation or mean that all disagreement is harmful.

4. Kobak et al. (2017), “Adapting to the Changing Needs of Adolescents.”
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5886742/

   **Design/claim limit:** Narrative developmental review. It supports the compatibility of positive engagement, supervision/guidance, open communication, and progressively greater autonomy. It does not validate the OWR mechanism or guarantee that one family practice changes outcomes.

5. Ehrlich et al. (2016), “When Parents and Adolescents Disagree About Disagreeing.”
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28581657/

   **Design/claim limit:** Two observational studies of conflict discussions. Parent and adolescent reports can differ, and observed communication related to differences between reports. It does not establish whose account is correct, infer intent, or prove that report differences cause harm.

6. Zahl-Olsen et al. (2023), “Effects of Emotionally Oriented Parental Interventions: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.”
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10374204/

   **Design/claim limit:** Review of varied intervention families, child ages, programs, outcomes, and study quality; 33 studies were assessed for bias and 29 outcome studies entered the meta-analysis. Small-to-medium post-treatment effects across included constructs do not validate VOICE → HEAR → CARRY or show that ordinary disagreement builds capability.

7. Raising Children Network, “Conflict management with pre-teens and teenagers.”
   https://raisingchildren.net.au/teens/communicating-relationships/communicating/conflict-management-with-teens

   **Authority/claim limit:** Expert guidance supports calm timing, listening, focus on one issue, and negotiation where appropriate. It is not intervention-effect proof, and negotiation is not required for safety, legal duties, or legitimate non-negotiable decisions.

8. American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren, “How to Communicate With and Listen to Your Teen.”
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Communicate-with-a-Teenager.aspx

   **Authority/claim limit:** Pediatric expert guidance supports non-judgmental listening and concrete communication rather than catastrophising or lecturing. It does not remove parental correction, consequences, supervision, or direct safety action.

9. American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren, “What to Expect When You Call 911.”
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Calling-911-How-and-When-to-Call-for-Help.aspx

   **Authority/scope limit:** US pediatric emergency guidance supports emergency medical help for severe illness or injury. The article translates this globally as contacting local emergency services and does not present 911 as a universal number.

10. World Health Organization, “Suicide,” questions and answers.
    https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/suicide

    **Authority/scope limit:** WHO advises contacting emergency services or a crisis line when someone is in immediate danger of self-harm. The article does not treat ordinary disagreement, an eye roll, one rude reply, or a request for time as self-harm evidence or an emergency.

11. WHO, “Assessing and supporting adolescents’ capacity for autonomous decision-making.”
    https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240039568

    **Authority/scope limit:** Health-care guidance supports progressive capability and adult scaffolding. It is broader than household conflict and does not transfer legal or household authority to a teenager.

12. UNICEF Parenting, “How to support your teen during stressful times.”
    https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/how-support-your-teen-during-stressful-times

    **Authority/claim limit:** General expert guidance supports calming first, listening to a teen’s view, avoiding angry discussion, and returning later. It is not a controlled test and does not validate the article’s mechanism.

## Claims and promises intentionally avoided

- No claim that all conflict is beneficial, educational, normal, or harmless.
- No claim that ordinary disagreement causes capability, or that hostility causes a specific outcome in one teenager.
- No claim that silence proves fear, surrender, manipulation, overload, avoidance, or understanding.
- No diagnosis of anxiety, defiance, trauma, neurodivergence, executive dysfunction, learning disability, family dysfunction, or abuse from one interaction.
- No promise that VOICE → HEAR → CARRY will reduce arguments, produce compliance, improve relationships, prevent withdrawal, or create respectful disagreement.
- No claim that the framework is validated, clinical, therapeutic, safeguarding, or suitable during an emergency.
- No claim that listening requires negotiation, agreement, removed consequences, delayed safety action, or equal parent/teen authority.
- No claim that a teen can carry a task without age-, disability-, development-, learning-, language-, or executive-function-aware support.
- No use of community language as factual authority, prevalence evidence, representative testimony, or proof of consensus.

## Community language — directional only

The Day 6 research map records normalized, anonymous wording from indexed Reddit discovery, including feeling out of options, wanting the arguing to stop, a teen shutting down after arguments, ending a discussion with no room for more argument, and ordinary requests repeatedly becoming arguments. These snippets shaped search language and recognisable household framing only.

The community items are directional discovery evidence, not representative samples, verified full accounts, verbatim testimony, factual authority, clinical evidence, prevalence data, or consensus. Two older traces were inaccessible as full pages and remain title/snippet-level wording only. No identities are retained and no claim in the article depends on these snippets.
