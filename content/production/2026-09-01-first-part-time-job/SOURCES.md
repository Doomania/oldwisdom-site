# Sources and claim limits

| Source | Claim-safe use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Mortimer (2010), *The Benefits and Risks of Adolescent Employment*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2936460/) | A U.S. longitudinal-study review supports treating adolescent employment as context-dependent: patterns, quality, prior characteristics, and selection into work matter. | It does not show that a first job, a given number of hours, or a parent conversation causes responsibility, confidence, grades, wellbeing, or job success. |
| [International Labour Organization, *Young Workers*](https://www.ilo.org/young-workers) | The ILO reports that young workers (15–24) can face higher non-fatal occupational-injury rates, with inexperience, limited training, limited hazard awareness, and lower bargaining power among risk factors. | A global aggregate does not mean every job is unsafe or identify the risks in one workplace. |
| [CDC/NIOSH, *Keeping Teens Safe and Healthy at Work*](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2019/working-teens.html) | U.S. public-health guidance supports discussing training, supervision, hazards, expected hours, injuries/emergencies, and rights; it states employers have the main responsibility for a safe workplace. | U.S.-specific and guidance-based; it does not establish a parental script as injury prevention or supply rules for other countries. |
| [UK HSE, *Young people at work*](https://www.hse.gov.uk/young-workers/employer/index.htm) | UK employer guidance supports the general principle that young people new to work need clear instruction, training, and supervision. | No UK legal requirements, ages, hours, or reporting routes are presented as global advice. |

## Editorial guidance boundaries

- `SIGN → SCAN → STEP BACK` is OWR editorial guidance for ordinary first-job preparation, not legal advice, a safety protocol, or a validated intervention.
- The parent helps the teen prepare questions and notice ordinary safety basics; the teen owns routine work communication. Employers remain responsible for workplace safety.
- Parents do not step back from immediate danger, illegal or exploitative conditions, or responsibilities they retain for safety, transport, or the household.
- Readers must use their local labour or child-employment authority for applicable ages, permitted tasks, pay, breaks, hours, permits, and reporting options.
