## Sources

1. **García et al. (2024), “Parental involvement in homework and academic achievement: A three-level meta-analysis.”**  
   https://scielo.isciii.es/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1886-144X2024000100001&lng=en&tlng=en  
   **Claim-safe use:** The meta-analysis covers 28 studies, 32 samples and 378,222 participants. It reports a weak overall association between parental homework involvement and lower achievement, with differences by involvement type; autonomy-supportive involvement was positively associated with achievement. The guide may distinguish the *quality* of involvement from its frequency.  
   **Limit:** Mostly correlational primary studies. It cannot show that stopping reminders improves results, establish a universal handover age, or justify abandoning support.

2. **Vasquez et al. (2016), “Autonomy-Supportive Parenting and Its Benefits: A Meta-Analysis of Parents’ Autonomy Support and Children’s Adjustment.”**  
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9329-z  
   **Claim-safe use:** Across 36 studies, parent autonomy support was related to achievement and adaptive psychosocial outcomes. It supports explaining that autonomy support is active parenting—acknowledging perspective and supporting self-endorsed action—not withdrawal or permissiveness.  
   **Limit:** “Related to” is not causal; findings span ages and contexts. Do not claim it prescribes one parenting style or applies specifically to boys 12–15.

3. **Hill & Tyson (2009), “Parental Involvement in Middle School: A Meta-Analytic Assessment of the Strategies That Promote Achievement.”**  
   https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015362  
   **Claim-safe use:** Supports keeping parents meaningfully involved through discussion of learning, goals and future pathways rather than making routine task-completion help the centre of involvement. The guide may say stepping back from daily reminders is not stepping away from school engagement.  
   **Limit:** Do not say parent discussion raises grades by itself or that homework help is always harmful.

4. **American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren.org, “Learning Problems in Teens.”**  
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/learning-disabilities/Pages/learning-problems-teens.aspx  
   **Claim-safe use:** Sustained homework difficulty or a marked change in functioning warrants curiosity and coordination with school or health professionals; learning and concentration problems can have multiple contributors.  
   **Limit:** Parent guidance, not a diagnostic tool. Do not infer ADHD, depression, learning disability, autism, or another condition from missed homework.

5. **American Academy of Pediatrics / HealthyChildren.org, “School Avoidance.”**  
   https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/emotional-problems/Pages/School-Avoidance.aspx  
   **Claim-safe use:** School avoidance, school-linked distress or physical complaints around attendance move the issue beyond ordinary homework ownership and warrant supportive assessment and appropriate local school/health contact.  
   **Limit:** Not evidence about ordinary procrastination or one late assignment; service routes vary by place.

## Claim boundaries

| Article claim | Support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| The quality of homework involvement matters more than simply increasing its frequency. | García et al.; Vasquez et al. | Association only; no claim that reminders cause poor outcomes. |
| A parent can stay engaged with learning while stepping back from daily monitoring. | Hill & Tyson; OWR editorial synthesis | Does not mean all task help is inappropriate or that a teen must manage alone. |
| The Ownership Handoff is a practical division of labour. | OWR editorial synthesis | Not a validated intervention and no outcome is promised. |
| A sustained change, access barrier, attendance concern or distress warrants re-engagement. | AAP guidance | Not diagnosis; appropriate support route depends on the context and locality. |

## Portfolio distinction

- `guide-teen-without-power-struggle`: broad communication choices inside ordinary household boundaries. This guide transfers the *monitoring role* through an **Ownership Handoff** and a weekly evidence review.
- `helpful-advice-sounds-like-criticism`: edits the content and tone of advice. This guide sets a repeatable boundary around reminders.
- `teen-wont-ask-teacher-for-help`: helps a teen make one routine teacher request. This guide decides when that contact becomes the teen’s responsibility and when a parent must appropriately re-enter.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim that repeated reminders cause dependence, anxiety, conflict or failure.
- No claim that a missed deadline is useful in every situation.
- No recommendation to step back before considering learning access, disability, anxiety, bullying, sleep, family stress, language, housing, caring duties, device access or unclear instructions.
- No gender-essential claim about boys.
- No claim that the guide improves grades, motivation, compliance or family harmony.
