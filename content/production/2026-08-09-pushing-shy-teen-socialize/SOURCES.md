## Claim audit

| Content claim used in article | Support | Wording guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Psychological control predicted perceived overcontrol, which predicted lower adolescent disclosure in a longitudinal eight-country study. | Selçuk et al. (2026) | The article names the study definition and does not equate psychological control with ordinary encouragement or rules. |
| Rule-setting did not predict disclosure over time; solicitation had more potential to support voluntary disclosure, with mixed findings. | Selçuk et al. (2026) | Presented as study-specific nuance, not a universal parenting law. |
| Parents should listen, avoid assumptions, and discuss what makes a situation stressful before forcing it. | NHS | Guidance, not a guaranteed treatment effect. |
| Listening without judgment or immediate alarm can keep a meaningful teen conversation open. | HealthyChildren.org / AAP communication guidance | Presented as communication guidance, not proof that one response causes or prevents disclosure. |
| Small, interest-led social steps and advance practice are preferable to indiscriminate exposure. | HealthyChildren.org / AAP | General parent guidance, not a prescribed treatment protocol. |
| Severe, persistent, worsening anxiety or anxiety interfering with daily life warrants additional help. | NHS; Nemours KidsHealth | Used as an escalation threshold, not a diagnosis. |
| Anxiety that interferes with usual activities warrants professional evaluation. | AACAP | Used as an escalation threshold, not to label ordinary shyness. |
| Shyness is not the same as social anxiety disorder. | Anna Freud | The article never diagnoses from reluctance or quietness. |
| Small supported steps can help young people practise coping; blanket avoidance is not the goal. | Nemours KidsHealth; NHS | The article does not prescribe unsupervised exposure therapy or promise a result. |
| Conversation pace may need to be set by a young person with social anxiety; dismissing fears can feel rejecting. | Anna Freud | Explicitly limited to guidance about social anxiety disorder, not every shy teen. |
| The Social Pressure Loop and Listen, Offer, Agree are OWR editorial frameworks. | Editorial synthesis from the evidence and v4 doctrine. | Clearly presented as reflection/action tools, not validated clinical models. |

## Adjacent-article differentiation

- `quiet-or-lonely-teen` asks what time alone means and uses Context, Change, and Cost.
- This guide asks what a parent’s response may teach after social difficulty is disclosed and uses the Social Pressure Loop plus Listen, Offer, Agree.
- Necessary overlap is limited to non-diagnosis, functional-impact thresholds, and safety escalation.
- The CTA points to the adjacent diagnostic-framing guide rather than repeating its mechanism.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim that ordinary encouragement causes secrecy, social anxiety, or lasting harm.
- No claim that every shy or introverted teen should control every family or school decision.
- No equivalence between introversion, shyness, solitude, avoidance, and social anxiety disorder.
- No promise that one script produces disclosure or confidence.
- No invented prevalence figure, testimonial, or quantified treatment outcome.
- No community post is treated as factual or representative evidence.
