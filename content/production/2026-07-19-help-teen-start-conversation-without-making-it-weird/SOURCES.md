## Brief claim audit

| Content claim used in batch | Support | Wording guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Feeling connected to school and peers matters for youth wellbeing and healthy development. | CDC school connectedness page; Jose et al. review on adolescent connectedness. | Framed as a general developmental and wellbeing point, not as a promise that one conversation will improve health outcomes. |
| Friendship usually grows out of familiarity, reciprocity, and shared context rather than instant charisma. | NCBI Bookshelf chapter on acquaintanceship, reciprocity, and sharing of interests in friendship formation. | Presented as a practical directional idea, not a hard universal law. |
| Asking questions, especially follow-up questions, tends to improve how conversations are received. | Huang et al. and Yeomans et al. conversation research. | Used to support "ask one small follow-up," not to claim any guaranteed social result. |
| Small, gradual self-disclosure helps adolescent relationship quality and reciprocal friendship. | Choukas-Bradley et al. systematic review. | The batch recommends shallow, ordinary self-disclosure only; it does not encourage oversharing or emotional dumping. |
| Confidence often follows reps instead of appearing first. | Inference drawn from the broader connectedness, practice, and conversation literature plus the site's product framing. | Marked as a practical coaching principle, not a direct quoted research conclusion. |

## Claims intentionally avoided

- No claim that this advice treats anxiety, depression, autism, ADHD, or any clinical condition.
- No promise that one script will make a teen popular, accepted, or successful.
- No invented testimonials, case studies, or quantified outcome claims.
- No long quotations from copyrighted sources.

## Review notes for human editor

- Re-check source accessibility and any wording tied to current platform policies before publishing.
- If the video title or article intro becomes more assertive during editing, re-confirm that the body copy still matches the evidence level above.
- Keep the quiz CTA truthful: it is an informational skills reflection, not a diagnostic or clinical assessment, and not a required route to a purchase.
