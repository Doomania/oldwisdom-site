# Source audit — phone takes the whole evening

**Status:** Draft evidence map, checked 2026-11-03. These sources support context, guidance and limits, not a tested OWR intervention. The teen-designed start and seven-day review are editorial suggestions, not proven treatment. The first independent review required changes; a targeted recheck of the revised draft passed at 20/20. Source-access limits remain recorded below.

## Claim-level evidence and boundaries

1. **Pew Research Center — Monica Anderson, Michelle Faverio and Eugenie Park, [How Teens and Parents Approach Screen Time](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/03/11/how-teens-and-parents-approach-screen-time/), March 11, 2024.** Directly read full report. Online survey of 1,453 paired US teens ages 13–17 and parents, Sept. 26–Oct. 23, 2023. Both parent and teen groups: 38% said they *at least sometimes* argue about how much time the teen spends on the phone; 10% in each group said *often*. Teens: 46% said their parent is at least sometimes distracted by a phone when they try to talk. Teens: 44% at least sometimes feel anxious without their smartphone, while 72% at least sometimes feel peaceful without it. **Use:** conflict is recognizable; ask what the phone is doing for this teen, and model attention. **Limit:** US self-report and cross-sectional; not proof that phones cause anxiety, parents cause conflict, or a given evening plan works. Do not say 38% argue nightly or regularly.

2. **American Academy of Pediatrics Council on Communications and Media, [How to Make a Family Media Plan](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/How-to-Make-a-Family-Media-Use-Plan.aspx), indexed update January 20, 2026.** Direct automated fetch returned **title only**; indexed passages described discussing family-fit rules with children and revisiting them. A QA reviewer reported an HTTP 200 page response, but that is not independent evidence that the relevant passages were directly inspected. **Use:** background guidance only; no direct AAP claim remains in the reader-facing draft. **Limit:** guidance is not proof that the proposed routine works.

3. **Moreno MA, Binger KS, Zhao Q and Eickhoff JC, [Effect of a Family Media Use Plan on Media Rule Engagement Among Adolescents](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7835954/), *JAMA Pediatrics* 2021;175(4):351–358, doi:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.5629.** The journal publisher returned 403; PMC showed a CAPTCHA to automated access. The [primary article's abstract via Europe PMC metadata API](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:33492346%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json&resultType=core) was directly read and returned HTTP 200. Randomized online trial of 1,520 English-reading parent–adolescent dyads (ages 12–17) comparing an AAP media plan with control. At about two months, adolescent-reported media-rule engagement did **not** significantly differ (between-group change −0.1, 95% CI −1.1 to 0.9). **Use:** counterweight to promises that writing a plan ensures adherence. **Limit:** the full article and attrition detail were not confirmed directly here; no attrition figure is used in the reader-facing draft. The trial did not test this guide's first move, weekly review, confiscation comparisons, or longer outcomes.

4. **US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, [Parent Training in Behavior Management](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/treatment/behavior-therapy.html), updated October 21, 2026.** Directly read. Its example tips include a consistent routine, managing distractions, brief clear directions, and breaking complex tasks into shorter steps; says to watch what works for the individual and discuss therapy with a clinician. **Use:** if ADHD makes initiation hard, make the start smaller, reduce distractions collaboratively, seek tailored support. **Limit:** much of the page, notably first-choice parent training for under-six children, concerns younger children. Not proof that this exact evening method treats ADHD in teens; do not diagnose ADHD from phone use.

5. **National Institute of Mental Health, [Children and Mental Health: Is This Just a Stage?](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/children-and-mental-health).** Directly read. Consider help if behavior/emotions persist for weeks, cause distress or interfere with school, home or friendships; seek immediate help for unsafe behavior or expressed desire to hurt self/others. **Use:** distinguish an ordinary scheduling problem from persistent anxiety, sleep/functioning changes or immediate safety concern; ask clinician/school support when relevant. **Limit:** does not diagnose this teen or link phone use to a disorder; urgent help follows risk, not a seven-day experiment.

6. **OWR primary book-page text — [The Discipline Playbook, sample](https://oldwisdomretold.com/books/the-discipline-playbook#sample), cross-checked against canonical checkout `books/the-discipline-playbook.html` and `content/books.json`.** Directly fetched live page and inspected local served-page source: both explicitly name “A phone or video keeps taking the whole evening”; `#sample` in local HTML is Chapter 1, “The Promise Nobody Heard,” whose selected excerpt names “one next action” and “a rough when.” **Use:** optional teen-owned chapter bridge only after the parent's complete answer. **Limit:** book copy is product/context, *not* independent evidence of the evening plan's efficacy; remote fetch does not prove interactive anchor navigation.

## Editorial source controls

- The reader-facing article must link evidence at each major factual claim; cite Pew precisely as *at least sometimes*, not every night. The family scenarios are illustrative, not surveyed cases. AAP and the Moreno trial remain audit context, not reader-facing claims, because direct full-text inspection could not be independently verified.
- No claim that co-design, a weekly review, device removal, or turning off notifications is superior or reliably changes behavior. Confiscation can remain a proportionate safety/household response; the title challenges nightly confiscation as the only default, not all device limits.
- Do not use Reddit as inspected evidence; direct access was blocked. Existing card's search-language phrases and simulated panel are planning hypotheses, not real parent quotes or demand data.
- Separate this evening-transition guide from voluntary shared leisure (`screen-free-activity`), privacy/safety phone checks (`should-i-check-my-teens-phone`), homework reminder handoff (`stop-reminding-homework`) and task-specific start (`task-start-runway`).
