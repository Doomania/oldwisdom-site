## Scope lock

This bundle addresses **assignment clarification only**: one task, instruction, deadline, or teacher-defined next step. It does not provide guidance on group participation, general confidence, school refusal, diagnosis, treatment, or broad parent–teen communication.

## Editorial synthesis disclosed

The following elements are OWR-created practical devices rather than findings tested by the cited sources:

- the name and sequence **TRY → ASK → NEXT**;
- all sample assignment questions, emails, spoken scripts, and follow-ups;
- the “five-minute handover”;
- choosing the least awkward workable channel;
- checking ordinary delivery details before following up;
- the parent escalation sequence for a routine unresolved assignment question; and
- the distinction between helping to draft and impersonating the teen.

These are framed as options and communication aids. The draft does not claim they cause higher grades, engagement, confidence, independence, compliance, or improved teacher behaviour.

## Portfolio distinctness

- **`quiet-teen-group-work`** addresses entering, contributing to, and recovering within a peer group task. This article does not coach peer participation or ask for group roles; it constructs a private clarification request about an academic task.
- **`helpful-advice-sounds-like-criticism`** includes a narrower ownership move around homework/email. This article’s complete reader job is identifying the stuck point, constructing TRY → ASK → NEXT, sending through a chosen teacher channel, using a follow-up, and escalating proportionately.
- **`guide-teen-without-power-struggle`** covers broad shared problem-solving. This article stays with one assignment, instruction, deadline, or next step.
- **`teen-wont-talk-after-school`** concerns home-arrival disclosure. This article does not seek a general account of the school day.

**Residual repetition risk:** Medium, controlled by the assignment-clarification scope lock and the prohibition on group-participation coaching.

## Claims intentionally avoided

- Asking for help guarantees better grades, confidence, engagement, independence, teacher support, or a deadline extension.
- Hesitation, missed work, quietness, or refusal establishes anxiety, ADHD, laziness, defiance, school refusal, or any diagnosis.
- A parent must remain silent when disability/access, persistent non-response, an unresolved assignment issue, or safeguarding requires adult action.
- Understood’s US disability-rights context establishes rights or accommodations in New Zealand.
- Oranga Tamariki is an appropriate route for ordinary teacher contact, schoolwork, attendance, discipline, or family disagreement.
- Any single contact method or escalation order applies to every school.

## Unresolved claim risk

**Low.** No material unsupported causal or diagnostic claim remains. The principal residual risk is that the suggested contact routes and escalation order vary by school; the article controls this with conditional wording and directs families to the school’s established teacher, year-level, dean, or learning-support routes. TRY → ASK → NEXT is clearly labelled here as editorial synthesis, not a research-tested protocol.
