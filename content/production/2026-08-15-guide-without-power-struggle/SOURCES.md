# Sources and claim limits

## Sources used

1. Beyers, W., Soenens, B., & Vansteenkiste, M. (2024). *Autonomy in adolescence: a conceptual, developmental and cross-cultural perspective.*
   https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024_BeyersSoenensVansteenkiste_EJODP.pdf

   **Claim limit:** A conceptual review explaining that autonomy is not simply independence or abandonment of support. It does not test the RESET → NOTICE → INVITE → RETURN framework or promise a result for one family.

2. Raising Children Network. *Listening and communicating with pre-teens and teenagers.*
   https://raisingchildren.net.au/teens/communicating-relationships/communicating/teen-communicating-relationships

   **Claim limit:** Practical expert guidance for listening and communication. It does not remove parent authority or make every household rule negotiable.

3. National Institute of Mental Health. *My Mental Health: Do I Need Help?*
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/my-mental-health-do-i-need-help

   **Claim limit:** General support guidance. The article only directs readers to local emergency services in immediate danger; it does not diagnose a teen from ordinary conflict.

## Claim boundaries

- The four-step flow is OWR editorial guidance, not a tested treatment or a guarantee of fewer arguments.
- An eye roll, silence, or pushback can have several meanings. The article does not diagnose intent, defiance, anxiety, trauma, or family dysfunction.
- Parents retain safety, health, legal, and proportionate household boundaries. A teenager having input on the practical next step is not a veto over the boundary.
