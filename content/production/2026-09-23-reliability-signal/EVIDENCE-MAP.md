# Evidence map — reliability signal parent plan

**Status:** DRAFT_ONLY. Direct source inspection recorded 2026-09-23.

## Authoritative sources

1. **Duckworth, Kirby, Gollwitzer & Oettingen (2014) — [From Fantasy to Action](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4106484/)** (randomized longitudinal school intervention; directly inspected).
   - Defines implementation intentions as if–then plans linking a cue to a specific goal-directed behaviour.
   - **Claim-safe use:** a teen can make one concrete cue → action plan rather than a broad promise to be more reliable.
   - **Limit:** combined MCII intervention, 77 fifth graders in one school; outcomes were school outcomes, not teen reputation, forgiveness, or trust.
2. **Wang et al. (2019) — [How Does the Transgressor Deal With Guilt](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00758/full)** (undergraduate laboratory trust-game study; directly inspected).
   - Finds trust repair after an apology also depends on recipient characteristics and emotion.
   - **Claim-safe use:** a brief ownership statement may be an opening; do not demand reassurance or promise repaired trust.
   - **Limit:** simulated adult single-violation task, not real teen peer/coach/teacher/work relationships.
3. **NIMH — [Teen Depression: More Than Just Moodiness](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/teen-depression)** (U.S. public-health authority; directly inspected).
   - Directs teens to trusted adults and professional support for concerning patterns; supplies urgent U.S. 988 information.
   - **Claim-safe use:** persistent withdrawal/low mood/functioning change or self-harm concern belongs outside a reliability plan.
   - **Limit:** no diagnosis from a few missed commitments; use local urgent support outside the U.S.

## Editorial mechanism and evidence limit
**One-Signal Repair Streak** is OWR editorial synthesis: choose one observable signal, set an if–then cue and backup, try it for the next three relevant commitments or seven days, review privately, and adjust after a miss.

Duckworth et al. only supports the narrower cue → action planning element. Wang et al. only supports the limit that an apology cannot compel a recipient’s trust. Neither source tests a teen reliability streak, reputation, forgiveness, or real-world trust repair. This guide must therefore present the plan as a next-commitment practice tool—not a reputation-repair protocol.

## Claim controls
- Do not say the plan proves a teen is not flaky, rebuilds trust, earns forgiveness, or repairs reputation in a set time.
- No parent should contact peers/teachers/coaches/supervisors secretly or promise outcomes for the teen.
- The plan does not waive attendance, deadline, workplace, or disciplinary requirements.

## Portfolio distinction
- `let-him-handle-it-badly`: broad handoffs/repair after a manageable mistake.
- `before-call-it-disrespect`: conduct interpretation and repair.
- This guide: a narrow ongoing pattern of observable follow-through across ordinary commitments.
