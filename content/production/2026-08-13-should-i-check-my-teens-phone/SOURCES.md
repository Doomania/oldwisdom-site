# Sources and Claim Audit

Research date: 2026-08-13
Audience: PARENT
Status: published bundle

## Source map

1. CDC, “Parental Monitoring” (22 November 2024).
   https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-youth-parent-resources/positive-parental-practices/parental-monitoring.html

   **Supports:** evolving monitoring, plans/whereabouts, clear expectations, regular check-ins, online-safety conversation, fair consequences, and the value of open caring relationships.
   **Limit:** associations and broad guidance do not prove one strategy causes honesty, prescribe reading all messages, or guarantee safety.

2. American Academy of Pediatrics, “Balancing Online Safety and Independence: Parental Monitoring by Age” (4 December 2025).
   https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/media-and-children/center-of-excellence-on-social-media-and-youth-mental-health/qa-portal/qa-portal-library/qa-portal-library-questions/balancing-online-safety-and-independence-parental-monitoring-by-age/

   **Supports:** adjusting monitoring to age, development, independence and concrete risk.
   **Limit:** guidance is not a law or universal access schedule; individual capability and risk differ.

3. Raising Children Network, “Privacy, Trust & Monitoring: 9–18 Years.”
   https://raisingchildren.net.au/pre-teens/communicating-relationships/family-relationships/privacy-trust-teen-years

   **Supports:** growing privacy, low-key routines, knowing practical safety facts, temporary closer monitoring after breaches, and reviewing trust.
   **Limit:** Australian parenting guidance; no promise that disclosure or monitoring causes trust.

4. NCMEC / NetSmartz, “Sextortion.”
   https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/topics/sextortion

   **Supports:** sextortion definition, trusted-adult help, non-blaming response, not paying or sending more content, platform reporting/privacy settings, and warning signs.
   **Limit:** US report pathways; suspicious screen behaviour alone does not prove exploitation.

5. StopBullying.gov, “Report Cyberbullying” (11 December 2024).
   https://www.stopbullying.gov/cyberbullying/how-to-report

   **Supports:** document dates/times/descriptions and save screenshots, emails and messages for a concrete cyberbullying incident.
   **Limit:** evidence preservation is not permission for open-ended surveillance or angry account takeovers.

6. NIMH, “5 Action Steps to Help Someone Having Thoughts of Suicide” (revised 2024).
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-to-help-someone-having-thoughts-of-suicide

   **Supports:** ask directly, listen, keep safe, connect responsible help, follow up; asking does not increase suicidal thoughts.
   **Limit:** US service details must be localised; a parent phone check is not a clinical assessment.

7. Baudat et al. (2022), adolescent disclosure, secrets and lying.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9090863/

   **Supports:** cautious distinction between disclosure, secrecy and lying; autonomy and connection can coexist.
   **Limit:** cross-sectional self-report study of 332 Swiss adolescents; no causation, universal prevalence, or phone-search rule.

8. New Zealand Police, “Report a crime.”
   https://www.police.govt.nz/advice-services/advice-victims/report-crime

   **Supports:** in New Zealand, calling 111 when someone is in immediate danger, a crime is occurring, or urgent Police help is needed.
   **Limit:** New Zealand-specific emergency routing; readers elsewhere need their local emergency number.

9. Child Welfare Information Gateway, “How to Report Child Abuse and Neglect.”
   https://www.childwelfare.gov/how-report-child-abuse-and-neglect/

   **Supports:** US readers reporting suspected abuse through the relevant child-protection pathway and calling 911 in an emergency.
   **Limit:** US-specific reporting routes; readers elsewhere need their local child-protection service.

## Claim audit

| Article move | Evidence basis | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy is not proof; monitoring can still be legitimate | AAP, Raising Children, CDC | No unlimited privacy or total-surveillance claim |
| Smallest targeted check | OWR editorial decision aid | Not validated, legal advice or digital forensics |
| Explain concern/scope when safe | CDC/AAP/Raising Children synthesis | Danger/evidence integrity can require protect-first action |
| Check together first and stop at scope | Editorial proportionality rule | Teen cannot veto a concrete safety check |
| Temporary closer checks after a lie | Raising Children and CDC | Defined reason, scope, review point and route back |
| Preserve cyberbullying evidence | StopBullying.gov | Concrete incident only; no redistribution of sexual images |
| Sextortion: do not pay/send more; get adult help | NCMEC | Local reporting pathways vary |
| Ask directly about suicide and connect help | NIMH | Not assessment/treatment; use local services |
| Immediate danger or urgent violence response | New Zealand Police | 111 applies in New Zealand only; use local emergency services elsewhere |
| Suspected abuse safeguarding route | Child Welfare Information Gateway | US reporting context only; use local child-protection service elsewhere |

## Claims intentionally avoided

- Hiding a screen proves lying, sexual behaviour, danger or addiction.
- Parents should never inspect a teen phone.
- Parents may secretly inspect everything at any time without relational cost.
- A teen’s refusal proves guilt.
- Monitoring apps prevent harm or produce honesty.
- One apology restores trust.
- THE SMALLEST CHECK detects crime, abuse, suicide risk or a diagnosis.
