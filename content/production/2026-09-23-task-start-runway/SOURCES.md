# Evidence map — task-start runway parent plan

**Status:** DRAFT_ONLY. Direct source inspection recorded 2026-09-23.

## Authoritative sources

1. **Harvard Center on the Developing Child — [A Guide to Executive Function](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resource-guides/guide-executive-function/)** (developmental research centre; directly inspected).
   - Says executive-function skills help people plan, focus attention, switch gears, and juggle tasks.
   - **Context-only use:** planning/focus language can describe the work demand without labelling the teen.
   - **Limit:** this is not evidence for Start-Runway Reset, a diagnosis, or a teen parent-intervention trial.
2. **Education Endowment Foundation — [Metacognition and self-regulated learning](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/metacognition)** (education evidence body; directly inspected).
   - Says strategies often involve planning, monitoring, and evaluating learning, and work best embedded in curriculum/subject lessons.
   - **Claim-safe use:** a finite, explicit first action is an educationally coherent planning prompt.
   - **Limit:** teacher/school guidance; it does not test a parent’s evening setup, higher attainment, or this OWR mechanism.
3. **Institute of Education Sciences — [Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED498555.pdf)** (U.S. government evidence guide; directly inspected).
   - Recommends helping students assess what they know and do not know to allocate study time; recommendation evidence level is Low.
   - **Claim-safe use:** for revision, identify one specific topic/question needing attention.
   - **Limit:** educator-facing, study-allocation guidance—not parent-run homework management or task-start treatment.
4. **National Center on Educational Outcomes — [The Basics of Accommodations and Modifications](https://nceo.umn.edu/docs/OnlinePubs/NCEOBrief33.pdf)** (University of Minnesota; directly inspected).
   - Says accommodations ensure equitable access and do not reduce/change learning expectations.
   - **Claim-safe use:** do not replace agreed disability/access support with this tool.
   - **Limit:** U.S. terminology/legal context.
5. **AAP / HealthyChildren — [Teen Mental Health: How to Know When Your Child Needs Help](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/teen/Pages/Mental-Health-and-Teens-Watch-for-Danger-Signs.aspx)** (U.S. pediatric guidance; directly inspected).
   - Identifies persistent changes and suicidal thoughts/actions as reasons for a broader conversation and urgent response.
   - **Claim-safe use:** persistent/severe change or immediate safety concern is outside an ordinary setup tool.
   - **Limit:** not a diagnostic checklist; one hard evening is insufficient.

## Editorial mechanism
**Start-Runway Reset** is OWR editorial synthesis: name one blocker, choose a finite visible first action, stage materials, and agree one proportionate distraction change for opening minutes. Teen chooses and does the academic task.

## Claim controls
- No claim to fix procrastination, motivation, executive function, grades, or performance.
- Not a daily parent supervision system; no surveillance or blanket phone confiscation.

## Portfolio distinction
- `stop-reminding-homework`: ongoing responsibility handoff and weekly review.
- `teen-wont-ask-teacher-for-help`: teacher clarification request.
- This guide: one low-friction starting line for tomorrow’s first five minutes.
