# Content card — task-start runway parent plan

**Date:** 2026-09-23
**Audience:** Busy parents of teens 10–16
**Status:** DRAFT_ONLY
**Slug:** task-start-runway

## Reader job
Support one ordinary revision, application, or assignment start problem without diagnosing procrastination or taking ownership of daily homework.

## Promise
**Fix tomorrow’s starting line tonight.** Identify one blocker, choose one finite visible first move, stage the materials, and agree one proportionate distraction change for opening minutes.

## Observable moment
A teen says “I can’t start,” circles the task, or repeatedly delays an otherwise ordinary piece of work.

## Belief shift
- **From:** I need to push harder or oversee the whole task.
- **To:** We can lower one practical barrier and let my teen try the first small move tomorrow.

## Mechanism
**Start-Runway Reset** — name blocker; visible finite first action; pre-position materials; one agreed distraction change. OWR editorial synthesis, not a treatment or productivity system.

## Required lanes
- Ordinary occasional friction: short setup experiment.
- Disability/access plan: use agreed supports; do not replace accommodation.
- Persistent/severe functioning or wellbeing change: broader conversation/support.
- Immediate safety concern: urgent local help.

## Scope lock
Not daily homework management, a phone-rule article, teacher-contact guide, learning diagnosis, or promise about focus/grades.

## Product relevance
**SOFT — War Playbook, Chapter 19.** Teen-facing friction-redesign capability; optional only after the complete standalone guide.

## Evidence required
The OWR mechanism is editorial synthesis, not an executive-function intervention. Harvard provides context-only planning/focus language; EEF and IES bound educational planning claims; NCEO and AAP supply access/wellbeing boundaries. See `EVIDENCE-MAP.md`.
