# Sources and Claim Audit

1. **CDC — Tips for encouraging independence (ages 11–17)**
   https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-teens/tips-encouraging-independence/index.html
   Claim-safe: involve older teens in expectations and decisions; listening while they reflect on emotions, decisions, and behaviour can help them connect decisions with consequences. This supports the article’s calm repair conversation, not a guarantee that a particular script changes behaviour.

2. **Smetana, Campione-Barr & Daddis (2004) — longitudinal study of adolescent decision-making autonomy**
   https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2004.00749.x
   PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15369523/
   Claim-safe: autonomy develops across types of decisions rather than as a single all-or-nothing transfer. Small observational sample; it does not prove causation or make high-consequence decisions teen-only.

3. **Bradshaw et al. (2025; online 2024) — meta-analysis of autonomy support and psychological control**
   https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0001389
   PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39052356/
   Claim-safe: parental autonomy support was associated with wellbeing and psychological control with ill-being across a large pooled literature. Association only; not proof that a single parental response creates an outcome.

4. **CDC — Parental monitoring**
   https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-youth-parent-resources/positive-parental-practices/parental-monitoring.html
   Claim-safe: parents retain expectations, awareness, and appropriate responses to broken rules as teens mature. This supports clear boundaries around high-risk choices, not a universal consequence.

## Boundaries

- “Let him handle it badly” applies only to reversible, age-appropriate mistakes with proportionate, repairable consequences.
- It does not apply to immediate danger; alcohol/drug use; driving or dangerous transport; sexual-image sharing, coercion, violence, abuse; significant health needs; major financial exposure; or legal or school-access problems.
- The article’s **practice mistake**, **repair conversation**, and **scaffold** are editorial family frameworks, not validated interventions.
