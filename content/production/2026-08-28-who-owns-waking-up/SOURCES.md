# Sources and Claim Audit

**Status:** `DRAFT_RESEARCHED` — no build, publication, distribution, or Pinterest action has occurred.

1. **AASM — Teen Sleep Duration Health Advisory**  
   https://aasm.org/advocacy/position-statements/teen-sleep-duration-health-advisory/  
   Claim-safe: ages 13–18 are recommended 8–10 hours’ sleep per 24 hours. Not evidence that an alarm handoff fixes sleep or attendance.

2. **AAP — School Start Times for Adolescents**  
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8194457/  
   Claim-safe: adolescence commonly brings later sleep timing. Population-level context only; not an explanation for any individual teen.

3. **U.S. Department of Education — Chronic Absenteeism**  
   https://www.ed.gov/teaching-and-administration/supporting-students/chronic-absenteeism  
   Claim-safe: chronic absenteeism is typically 10% or more of school days missed. Seek school support earlier if a concerning pattern builds.

4. **CDC — Sleep in Middle and High School Students**  
   https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/healthyschools/features/students-sleep.htm  
   Claim-safe: many students do not get needed sleep; do not infer a particular child’s cause, mood, attendance, or performance.

## Boundaries

- The **Alarm Handoff** is an editorial family framework, not a proven intervention.
- Adult support remains necessary for disability/accommodation needs, safety, access barriers, new or severe functional change, school distress, bullying concerns, and escalating attendance problems.
- No diagnosis, guarantee, or moral explanation for difficult waking is made.
