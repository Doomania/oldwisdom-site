## Authoritative and empirical sources used

1. National Institute of Mental Health, “5 Action Steps to Help Someone Having Thoughts of Suicide” (revised 2024).
   https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-to-help-someone-having-thoughts-of-suicide

   **Scope limit:** Supports asking directly about suicide, listening without judgment, connection to capable support, and the statement that asking does not increase suicidal thoughts or behaviour. It does not make a teenager a clinical risk assessor. US service details are location-specific.

2. 988 Lifeline, “Youth.”
   https://988lifeline.org/help-yourself/youth/

   **Scope limit:** Supports not keeping suicide a peer-only secret and involving a trusted adult. This is a US service; the article tells readers elsewhere to use local services.

3. YoungMinds, “Supporting a friend with their mental health.”
   https://www.youngminds.org.uk/young-person/supporting-a-friend-with-their-mental-health/

   **Scope limit:** Youth guidance supports breaks, limits, wider support, and telling a trusted adult about danger. It does not prove a boundary will improve a friendship or prevent harm.

4. NSPCC, “What to do if a child reveals abuse.”
   https://www.nspcc.org.uk/keeping-children-safe/reporting-abuse/what-to-do-child-reveals-abuse/

   **Scope limit:** Supports calm listening, explaining next steps, and reporting safeguarding concerns. It is adult-focused UK guidance and does not instruct a teen to investigate or confront an alleged abuser.

5. NHS, “Poisoning” and “First aid.”
   https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/
   https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/first-aid/

   **Scope limit:** Supports immediate medical escalation for overdose/poisoning, unresponsiveness, severe bleeding, or breathing danger. UK numbers and pathways are location-specific; teens are not assigned treatment or transport responsibility.

6. New Zealand Police, “111 Emergency Services.”
   https://www.police.govt.nz/call-111/

   **Scope limit:** Supports emergency action for serious or immediate danger, threats, or violence. The article uses location-neutral emergency wording and does not advise physical intervention.

7. Oranga Tamariki—Ministry for Children, “Worried about a child? Tell us.”
   https://www.orangatamariki.govt.nz/worried-about-a-child-tell-us/

   **Scope limit:** Supports reporting suspected abuse or neglect and using emergency police for immediate danger. New Zealand processes are location-specific; follow local law and services.

8. Spendelow, Simonds and Avery (2017), systematic review and meta-analysis of co-rumination; 38 cross-sectional studies across mixed child, adolescent, and young-adult ages.
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27215974/
   https://doi.org/10.1002/cpp.2023

   **Design/claim limit:** Repeated problem talk had small-to-moderate associations with internalising outcomes and can include closeness or friendship satisfaction. Cross-sectional associations do not establish causation and cannot diagnose one friendship.

9. Starr and Davila (2008/2009), one-year study of 83 early-adolescent girls.
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2652577/

   **Design/claim limit:** Co-rumination was linked at baseline with closeness and depressive symptoms but did not predict later depressive-symptom change. The small girls-only regional sample is not universal evidence.

10. Campos-Castillo et al. (2021), qualitative study using hypothetical scenarios with 43 Latinx adolescents aged 13–17 in Milwaukee.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8482171/

    **Design/claim limit:** Supports helping a teen choose an adult based on safety, proximity, experience, and ability to act, while recognising fear of punishment. It does not establish prevalence or a universal adult hierarchy.

## Claims and promises intentionally avoided

- No diagnosis or label such as toxic, manipulative, codependent, attention-seeking, or mentally ill.
- No claim that frequent crisis talk causes anxiety or depression.
- No universal message count, time threshold, or reciprocity rule.
- No promise that CARE → CHECK → CONNECT → STEP BACK prevents suicide, fixes a friendship, or guarantees disclosure.
- No automatic friendship ban, blanket surveillance, public sharing, or confrontation of an alleged abuser.
- No instruction for a teen to assess, treat, transport, disarm, investigate, or physically intervene alone.
- No implication that US 988 or any country-specific number works worldwide.
